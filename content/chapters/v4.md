Who counts as Black depends on who is counting. In 1924 [[what-black-means|Virginia's Racial Integrity Act]] called white only a person with "no trace whatsoever of any blood other than Caucasian". Brazil's 1976 household survey, which let people name their own colour, recorded 136 terms, from morena to clara.

The diaspora reached the Russian court. Around 1705 a kidnapped African boy was brought to Peter the Great, and the tsar became his godfather and sent him to France to study military engineering. As [[gannibal|Abram Petrovich Gannibal]] he rose to general-in-chief; his great-grandson was Alexander Pushkin.

In Salvador, around 1830, a house for the orixás was founded behind the Barroquinha church, and from it descend [[candomble|the city's most famous candomblé temples]]. When the anthropologist Ruth Landes worked in them in 1938–39, she found them led by women.

[[capoeira|Capoeira]] is a fight and a dance at once, played in a circle to the single-string berimbau. Brazil's 1890 penal code sent its players to prison; in 2014 UNESCO inscribed the capoeira circle as intangible heritage.

Migrants carried [[samba|samba de roda]] south to Rio, where Bahian "aunts" led the Black neighbourhood around Praça Onze. At the house of Tia Ciata a song called Pelo Telefone took shape; Donga registered it in 1916, though other regulars said the tune was collective.

In São Paulo, [[carolina-maria-de-jesus|Carolina Maria de Jesus]], who had two years of school, collected waste paper for a living and raised three children in a favela shack. And she wrote. In 1960 her diary came out as Quarto de Despejo, "Room of Garbage".

Some of the diaspora sailed back to Africa. Settlers sent by the American Colonization Society first sailed in 1820, and in 1847 [[liberia|Liberia]] became a republic. In 1930 an international commission found its officials shipping forced labour to Fernando Po, in conditions "scarcely distinguishable from slave raiding".

In the same country, in the early 1830s, Mọmọlu Duwalu Bukẹlẹ gave the Vai language [[vai-script|a script of its own]]. The idea, he later said, came to him in a dream, in which a messenger showed him a book. The script went to work at once, in letters, accounts and records of trade.

In Cameroon a king did the same by design. Ibrahim Njoya began [[bamum|the Bamum script]] with hundreds of signs for things and actions and, through six revisions, simplified it into a working system. In Foumban it served for letters, records, medicine and a history of his people.

In Port of Spain young men hammered discarded oil drums into tuned notes and made [[steelpan|the steelpan]]. In 1951 a Trinidad steel orchestra played the Festival of Britain, and in 2024 Parliament voted the steelpan the national instrument.
