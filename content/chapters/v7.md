The future, too, was made out of the past. [[sun-ra|Sun Ra]], born Herman Poole Blount in Birmingham, Alabama, in 1914, legally took his new name in Chicago in 1952 and built his band there, the Arkestra. In the early 1990s the critic Mark Dery named the idea: Afrofuturism.

[[octavia-butler|Octavia E. Butler]], raised in Pasadena by a mother who worked as a maid, brought slavery into science fiction through time travel in Kindred (1979). She wrote affirmations to herself that her books would be bestsellers, and in 2020, fourteen years after her death, one reached the New York Times list.

On 11 August 1973 a high-school girl, [[cindy-campbell|Cindy Campbell]], threw a party in a Bronx recreation room because she needed money for new school clothes. She wrote the invitations on index cards and asked her brother Clive to play the music.

Clive, born in Kingston and raised in its sound-system culture, became DJ Kool Herc. He played two copies of one funk or soul record on two turntables, switching between them so the drum break ran as long as he liked. That night is commonly called the birth of [[hip-hop|hip-hop]].

A break could travel the world. In 1969 the Winstons put [[amen-break|"Amen, Brother"]] on a B-side, with Gregory C. Coleman on drums. Its few bars were looped in hip-hop, then sped up into jungle and drum and bass, and neither Coleman nor the band's leader received sample royalties.

Machines from abroad were rebuilt too. In 1982 Afrika Bambaataa cut [[planet-rock|"Planet Rock"]], setting Kraftwerk's "Trans-Europe Express" on a Roland TR-808. Kraftwerk were angry, and the label, Tommy Boy, paid them for every copy.

In Detroit the machines became a whole music. Juan Atkins, Derrick May and Kevin Saunderson met at high school in Belleville, outside the city, and became the Belleville Three of [[detroit-techno|Detroit techno]]. In 1991, in Berlin, the new Tresor label opened with Underground Resistance's "X-101".

The future was built in hardware as well. [[jerry-lawson|Jerry Lawson]] grew up in Queens building ham radios and became one of the first Black engineers in Silicon Valley. At Fairchild his team made the Channel F (1976), the first home console with interchangeable game cartridges.

And in letters. In 1989 in Nzérékoré, Guinea, two brothers, Ibrahima Barry, 14, and Abdoulaye, 10, gave the Fulani language [[adlam|Adlam]], a script of its own. Its name also stands for a phrase meaning "the alphabet that protects my people from vanishing"; in 2016 Unicode encoded it.

In Lagos the future came on videotape. Kenneth Nnebue put an original story onto VHS: Living in Bondage (1992), shot in Igbo for 150,000 naira and remembered as the first blockbuster of [[nollywood|Nollywood]].
