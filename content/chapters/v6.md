Rock and roll had a godmother before it had a name. In 1938 [[rosetta-tharpe|Sister Rosetta Tharpe]] brought her gospel guitar to the stage of the Cotton Club in Manhattan. In 1964, at a disused Manchester railway station, she played electric guitar for television.

The songs travelled faster than the credit. In 1953 [[big-mama-thornton|Willie Mae "Big Mama" Thornton]]'s "Hound Dog" went to number one on the R&B chart; three years later Elvis Presley's version brought him fame and a fortune.

In August 1955 [[emmett-till|Emmett Till]], a Chicago boy of fourteen visiting family in Mississippi, was taken from his great-uncle's house, and his body was found in the Tallahatchie River. His mother held an open-casket funeral and let Jet photograph her son.

{male} Other harm was done quietly, in the name of medicine. In 1932 [[tuskegee|a US Public Health Service study]] enrolled 399 Black men with syphilis in Macon County, Alabama, and when penicillin became the standard cure, they were not offered it. It ended only in 1972, after a leak to the press.

{female} Other harm was done quietly, in the name of medicine. In 1951 doctors at Johns Hopkins Hospital kept a sample of tumour cells from [[henrietta-lacks|Henrietta Lacks]], and no one asked her. Her cells became HeLa, the first immortalised human cell line, used in testing for the polio vaccine, while her family was left out.

Writers took their stories back. [[achebe|Chinua Achebe]], born in Ogidi, wrote Things Fall Apart to tell an Igbo village's story from the inside, and nearly lost it when the London agency he had mailed the manuscript to went silent.

In Kenya the question was which language to tell them in. After peasants and workers at Kamiriithu staged a Gikuyu play he had co-written, [[ngugi|Ngũgĩ wa Thiong'o]] was held without charge for a year, and in prison he wrote his first Gikuyu novel on toilet paper.

Others answered with an alphabet. Around 1949 Souleymane Kanté, living in Bingerville in Côte d'Ivoire, read, the story goes, that Africans had no writing of their own. His answer was [[nko|N'Ko]], and its users still mark 14 April 1949 as Alphabet Day.

In Lagos the answer was a groove. In 1969 Fela Ransome-Kuti spent months in Los Angeles, where the activist Sandra Izsadore introduced him to Malcolm X and the Black Power thinkers; at home he built [[afrobeat|Afrobeat]].In 1977 soldiers stormed his commune and threw his mother from a window.

In London the reply was a carnival. Deported from the United States in 1955, [[claudia-jones|Claudia Jones]] met the race riots of 1958 with a Caribbean carnival at St Pancras Town Hall, and such indoor carnivals fed the Notting Hill Carnival.
