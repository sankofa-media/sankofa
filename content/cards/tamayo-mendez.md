Arnaldo Tamayo Méndez was born in Guantánamo, in eastern Cuba, in 1942. Orphaned as a small child and adopted, he grew up poor; from the age of 13 he worked as a shoeshine boy and a carpenter's helper. After the revolution of 1959 he joined the Cuban air force and became a fighter pilot.

The Soviet Interkosmos programme flew guest cosmonauts from allied countries to its space stations, and in 1978 Tamayo was chosen as Cuba's candidate. He trained at Star City outside Moscow. On 18 September 1980 he lifted off in Soyuz 38 with the Soviet commander Yuri Romanenko. The two docked with the Salyut 6 station, worked on experiments aboard, and landed on 26 September, almost eight days later. In Moscow he received the Order of Lenin and the title Hero of the Soviet Union, and at home Fidel Castro made him the first Hero of the Republic of Cuba.

He was the first Cuban, the first Latin American and the first person of African descent to fly in space, three years before Guion Bluford became the first African American in space in 1983. Tamayo went back to the air force, rose to brigadier general, ran Cuba's civil defence organisation and sat as a deputy in the National Assembly.
