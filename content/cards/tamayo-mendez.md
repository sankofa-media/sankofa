Arnaldo Tamayo Méndez was born in Guantánamo, in eastern Cuba, in 1942. Orphaned as a small child, he was adopted at the age of one and grew up poor; as a boy he worked as a shoeshine. After the revolution of 1959 he joined the Cuban air force and became a fighter pilot.

In the late 1970s the Soviet Union's Interkosmos programme was flying cosmonauts from allied countries to its space stations, and Tamayo was chosen as Cuba's candidate. He trained at Star City outside Moscow. On 18 September 1980 he lifted off in Soyuz 38 with the Soviet commander Yuri Romanenko. The two docked with the Salyut 6 station, worked on experiments aboard, and landed on 26 September, almost eight days later. In Moscow he received the Order of Lenin and the title Hero of the Soviet Union, and at home Fidel Castro made him the first Hero of the Republic of Cuba.

He was the first Cuban, the first Latin American and the first person of African descent to fly in space, three years before Guion Bluford became the first African American astronaut in 1983. Tamayo went back to the air force, rose to brigadier general, ran Cuba's civil defence organisation and in 2018 was elected to the National Assembly.
