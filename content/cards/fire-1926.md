FIRE!! was a literary and art magazine made in Harlem in 1926 by the youngest generation of the Harlem Renaissance. That year Wallace Thurman, [[hurston|Zora Neale Hurston]], Aaron Douglas, John P. Davis, Richard Bruce Nugent, Gwendolyn Bennett and Langston Hughes set out to publish what Hughes later called "a Negro quarterly of the arts". As he remembered it, the name meant the magazine would burn up the old, dead conventional ideas, and it would print what the limited pages of The Crisis and Opportunity would not. Thurman edited it.

The single issue came out in November 1926 in a bright red cover drawn by Douglas, who also painted [[aspects-of-negro-life|Aspects of Negro Life]]. Inside were stories, plays and poems by Hurston, Hughes, Countee Cullen and others, and Nugent's "Smoke, Lilies and Jade", a story with homosexuality as its central theme. The white press barely noticed it, and the older Black intellectuals, W. E. B. Du Bois among them, dismissed it.

The money ran out at once. Then several hundred unsold copies, stored in a basement, burned in an actual fire. No second issue followed.

Original copies are now rare treasures; facsimiles keep it in print. Its one issue said what the young wanted: to write Black life as they saw it, without permission.
