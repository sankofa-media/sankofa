Touki Bouki is a Senegalese feature of 1973, the first by Djibril Diop Mambéty. Its Wolof title means "the journey of the hyena". Mambéty built it from jump cuts and clashes of sound and image, drawing on the French New Wave and the avant-garde while telling a story entirely his own.

Mory is a cowherd who rides around Dakar on a motorbike with a cow's skull and horns mounted on the front; Anta is a student. They dream of Paris, and on the soundtrack Josephine Baker keeps singing "Paris, Paris". They try one scheme after another to raise the money and at last book a passage on a ship to France. At the last moment Mory turns back, and the ship sails with Anta aboard.

Mambéty made only one more feature, Hyènes (1992), from Friedrich Dürrenmatt's play The Visit, and died in 1998. In 2008 Martin Scorsese's World Cinema Foundation restored Touki Bouki and showed it at Cannes. The film now stands in Sight and Sound's critics' list of the hundred greatest films ever made.
