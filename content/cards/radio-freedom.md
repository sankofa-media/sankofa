Radio Freedom was the radio station of the African National Congress, the movement that apartheid South Africa had outlawed. It was born in 1963, when the ANC's leaders were going underground. From a secret hideout near Johannesburg, Walter Sisulu spoke into the microphone: "I speak to you from somewhere in South Africa... Our house is on fire." The police soon raided the hideout, and the station went silent.

It returned in exile. Radio Tanzania gave it airtime first; later the national broadcasters of Zambia, Ethiopia and Madagascar opened their transmitters to it, and Lusaka, the ANC's headquarters in exile, became its home. Its announcers were drawn from the movement's own ranks, many of them fighters of its armed wing, and some trained in Eastern Europe. Every programme began with a burst of automatic gunfire and went on to news, political commentary and songs. At home, people tuned in on shortwave.

For almost thirty years the station was a thread between the exiled movement and people inside the country, although listening to it could cost a South African up to eight years in prison. When apartheid began to give way and the exiles went home, the station quietly signed off, in August 1991.
