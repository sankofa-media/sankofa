The Balobedu, or Lobedu, live in the hills around Modjadjiskloof in Limpopo, in north-eastern South Africa. Their hereditary ruler, the Modjadji, a queen, has long been famous across the region as a maker of rain. In the 1930s the anthropologists Eileen and Jack Krige lived among the Lobedu, and in 1943 they published their study of them, The Realm of a Rain-Queen.

Women had ruled the Balobedu for about two centuries when Queen Makobo Modjadji VI died in 2005. Her heir, her daughter Masalanabo, was a child, and the girl's uncle, Prince Mpapatla, ruled as regent. Not everyone accepted her succession: in October 2022 a rival royal council crowned her elder brother, Prince Lekukela, in a traditional ceremony.

Masalanabo came of age in January 2023. On 13 December 2024 President Cyril Ramaphosa recognised her as Queen Modjadji VII, holder of the only queenship legally recognised in South Africa. Her brother and her uncle challenged her recognition in court, and in 2026 the case was still before the High Court in Pretoria. An office that brings the rain is also an office of a modern state.
