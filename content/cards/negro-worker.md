The Negro Worker was the paper of the International Trade Union Committee of Negro Workers, which the Communist International set up in 1928 to reach Black workers in Africa and the Americas. In July 1930 the committee held its first international conference in Hamburg, where the Comintern had its Western Secretariat and a port with sea links to the Americas and the coast of Africa. The paper was edited there by George Padmore, born Malcolm Nurse in Trinidad.

Colonial governments tried to keep it out. In April 1932 the governor of Trinidad and Tobago, Padmore's home colony, banned its import, and censorship orders against it spread across Africa and the West Indies. In February 1933, weeks after Hitler came to power, the Hamburg office was raided and Padmore was deported to Britain. That year he left the paper, accusing Moscow of dropping the colonial cause to court Britain and France, and in February 1934 he was expelled from the Communist movement. The committee faded and was dissolved by 1937–38.

Padmore turned to Pan-Africanism. He became a mentor to Kwame Nkrumah, moved to independent Ghana at his invitation and died in 1959. The Hamburg paper had linked Black radicals across three continents.
