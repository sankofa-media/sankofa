In 1920 Pura Belpré, a university student from Cidra, Puerto Rico, came to New York for her sister's wedding and stayed. Like many Puerto Rican women in the city, she first worked in the garment industry. In 1921 the 135th Street branch of the New York Public Library, in the middle of Harlem, took her on as a Spanish-speaking assistant: she was the first Puerto Rican the library hired. The Harlem Renaissance was under way around her branch, and she worked there with Black, Puerto Rican and immigrant readers alike.

In a library storytelling class she wrote her first story, Pérez and Martina, the love of a mouse and a cockroach, from a Puerto Rican folktale. It came out as a book in 1932, the first Puerto Rican folktale published in the United States. She ran bilingual story hours and travelled the city telling Puerto Rican tales, in Spanish and English, with puppets. In 1943 she married Clarence Cameron White, an African American violinist and composer, and later left the library to travel with him and write. She died in 1982.

Since 1996 the American Library Association has given the Pura Belpré Award to Latino writers and illustrators whose books celebrate Latino culture for children.
