In the Chicago of the 1930s, restrictive covenants barred Black families from buying or renting homes in whole neighbourhoods. Carl and Nannie Hansberry, a South Side family, defied one by moving into a white area. The Illinois courts upheld the covenant; the family took the case to the US Supreme Court and won, in Hansberry v. Lee, in 1940.

Their daughter Lorraine, born in Chicago in 1930, turned that history into A Raisin in the Sun, its title taken from Langston Hughes's poem "Harlem", which asks what happens to a dream deferred. The Younger family share a small South Side apartment and wait for the life-insurance cheque that follows the father's death. Mama wants a house; her son Walter Lee wants to start a business. When Mama buys a house in a white neighbourhood, the family meets the resistance the Hansberrys had met.

The play opened at the Ethel Barrymore Theatre on 11 March 1959, with Sidney Poitier as Walter Lee. It was the first play by a Black woman produced on Broadway and the first there with a Black director, Lloyd Richards.

Hansberry died in 1965, aged 34. A Raisin in the Sun has never left the stage: it became a film in 1961 and returned to Broadway in 2004 and 2014.
