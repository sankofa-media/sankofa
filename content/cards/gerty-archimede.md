Guadeloupe was still a French colony when Gerty Archimède, born in Morne-à-l'Eau in 1909, took up the law. In 1939, on the eve of the Second World War, she became the first woman admitted to the bar in Guadeloupe, the first woman lawyer in the French Antilles and, by several accounts, the first Black woman lawyer in France. She soon made her name in criminal defence.

In 1945 she was elected to Guadeloupe's general council, and in 1946 voters sent her to the National Assembly in Paris as a Communist deputy. There she worked to open the judicial professions to women. At home she founded the Guadeloupe branch of the Union of French Women, which grew into the Union of Guadeloupean Women and fought for women's rights.

Back at the bar she rose to lead Guadeloupe's lawyers as their bâtonnière. When the American activist Angela Davis and her companions were detained by the French authorities in Guadeloupe while passing through, Archimède took up her defence. She died in 1980. Guadeloupe's first woman lawyer had become the head of its bar.
