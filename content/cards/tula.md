In 1795, French revolutionary armies helped turn the Netherlands into the Batavian Republic, and France had already abolished slavery in its colonies. News of the French Revolution and of the uprising in Saint-Domingue reached the Dutch island of Curaçao. Tula, enslaved on the Knip plantation, drew the obvious conclusion.

On 17 August 1795 the enslaved people of Knip, led by Tula, refused to work. They marched across the island, freeing prisoners on the way. The colony sent a priest, Jacobus Schinck, to talk them down. Tula told him that they wished no one harm and sought only their freedom: the French Black people had been freed, Holland was under the French, so they too must be free. Did not all people descend from Adam and Eve, he asked.

The answer was gunfire. Troops crushed the revolt, and its leaders were captured. On 3 October 1795 Tula was tortured and executed, and his body was put on display as a warning.

Schinck's notes are almost the only record of Tula's own words, which is why they are so precious. In 2010 Curaçao declared Tula a national hero, and in October 2023 the government of the Netherlands formally rehabilitated him. The [[haitian-revolution|Haitian Revolution]] had found an echo.
