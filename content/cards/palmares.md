Quilombo dos Palmares was a refuge of people who escaped slavery in colonial Brazil. From around 1600, runaways from the sugar plantations of Pernambuco built a network of villages around the Serra da Barriga, in present-day Alagoas. For almost a century they beat back Dutch and Portuguese expeditions. King Ganga Zumba accepted a peace deal from the governor; Zumbi refused it, the deal collapsed, and Zumbi took the lead. In 1694 a force under Domingos Jorge Velho, a hired frontier fighter, or bandeirante, captured their capital, Macaco. Zumbi escaped and fought on until one of his own men betrayed him: on 20 November 1695 he was killed and his head sent to Recife. Brazil's Black movement made that date its own, and law 14.759 of December 2023 made it a national holiday, the Dia de Zumbi e da Consciência Negra. Palmares became the most famous of Brazil's quilombos and the model through which historians and anthropologists came to see them all.
