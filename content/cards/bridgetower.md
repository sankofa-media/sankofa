George Bridgetower was a violin virtuoso of African descent, born in Biała in Galicia to a Black father and a European mother. A child prodigy, he played in Paris in 1789 and then won the patronage of the Prince of Wales, the future George IV, in London. In the spring of 1803 he was in Vienna, where he befriended Beethoven, and the violin sonata everyone now knows as the "Kreutzer", Op. 47, was written for him. On 24 May 1803 he and Beethoven gave its first performance. On the autograph, now in the Beethoven-Haus in Bonn, Beethoven wrote a joking Italian dedication to "il Mulatto Brischdauer gran pazzo e compositore mulattico", the mulatto Bridgetower, great madman and mulatto composer. Then the friendship broke, reportedly over a woman, and when the sonata was printed in 1805 it was dedicated to the French violinist Rodolphe Kreutzer.

Bridgetower went on performing and teaching in England and took a music degree at Cambridge in 1811, but he slipped into obscurity and died in Peckham, in south London, in 1860. One of the best-known violin sonatas still carries the name of the wrong man. Bridgetower stands with other men of African descent who made their names in late eighteenth-century Europe, such as [[saint-georges|Joseph Bologne, Chevalier de Saint-Georges]] and [[general-dumas|General Thomas-Alexandre Dumas]].
