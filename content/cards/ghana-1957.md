On 6 March 1957 the British colony of the Gold Coast, in West Africa, became the independent nation of Ghana, hailed as the first colony south of the Sahara to break free. Its leader was Kwame Nkrumah. He had studied at Lincoln University and the University of Pennsylvania, and in 1949 he founded the Convention People's Party to demand self-government. The British imprisoned him in 1950. In 1951 his party won the colony's general election in a landslide, he was released from prison to lead the government, and in 1952 he became prime minister.

At the stroke of midnight Nkrumah proclaimed independence in Accra. Washington sent Vice-President Richard Nixon to lead its delegation. Martin Luther King Jr. came too, fresh from the [[montgomery-bus-boycott|Montgomery bus boycott]]. At a reception King reportedly asked Nixon to come to Alabama, where Black citizens were seeking the same freedom the Gold Coast was celebrating. Free Ghana drew Black American leaders and icons; [[malcolm-x|Malcolm X]] was among them.

Nkrumah's rule hardened: he banned the other parties, and in 1966 he was overthrown in a coup while abroad. He died in exile in 1972. Yet the midnight in Accra came first, and in [[year-of-africa-1960|1960]] seventeen more African colonies followed it to independence.
