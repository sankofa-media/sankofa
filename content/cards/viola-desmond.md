In the 1940s Black Nova Scotians routinely met racism, from segregated schools to segregated cinemas. Viola Desmond, born in Halifax in 1914, had trained as a beautician in Montreal and New York, since few schools in Canada would take a Black student, and ran her own beauty studio and school in Halifax, selling products made for Black women.

On 8 November 1946 her car broke down in New Glasgow on a business trip to Sydney, and she went to the Roseland Theatre to see a film. She asked for a main-floor seat and was sold a balcony ticket, the section kept for Black patrons. She sat downstairs anyway. She was dragged out, held in a cell overnight, and the next morning convicted of evading tax: one cent, the difference between the two seats. The Nova Scotia Association for the Advancement of Coloured People backed her appeal, and the Supreme Court of Nova Scotia turned it down in 1947.

Desmond died in New York in 1965. In 2010 Nova Scotia granted her a free pardon and apologised, and on 19 November 2018 the Bank of Canada issued a ten-dollar note with her portrait, the first time a Canadian woman appeared on her own on a Canadian banknote.
