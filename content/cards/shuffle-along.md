Shuffle Along was a musical comedy written, composed and performed by Black artists, and it opened in New York on 23 May 1921, at the 63rd Street Music Hall. The comedians Flournoy Miller and Aubrey Lyles wrote the book, a farce about two crooked candidates for mayor of "Jimtown"; Eubie Blake wrote the music and Noble Sissle the lyrics. No show wholly by Black artists had been a success on Broadway for about a decade.

The plot was a pretext; the songs were the event: "I'm Just Wild About Harry" became the big hit. In "Love Will Find a Way" two young Black lovers sang a sentimental ballad, something the American stage had kept out of bounds for Black characters except as caricature. The theatre broke another custom too. Black patrons were usually sent up to the top balcony; here the box office sold them seats in the orchestra.

The show made stars. Florence Mills stepped into a lead role and the city fell for her. When the Broadway run closed in July 1922, the show went on tour, and a teenage Josephine Baker danced in its chorus.

Shuffle Along brought Black musicals back to Broadway and helped launch the Harlem Renaissance; from it run the roads to [[blackbirds-1926|Blackbirds]] in London and [[revue-negre|La Revue nègre]] in Paris.
