"Yé ké yé ké" is a 1987 dance single by Mory Kanté, a singer and kora player from Guinea. The kora is the West African harp of the griots, hereditary musicians whose songs carry news and history, and Kanté was born into a griot family.

He was sent to Mali to continue his training, and in Bamako he joined the Rail Band; when its star singer Salif Keita left, Kanté took over as lead vocalist. Later he settled in Paris. On "Yé ké yé ké", released as a single from his album Akwaba Beach, he played an amplified kora backed by the instruments of Western pop, a sound that earned him the nickname of the electronic griot.

The single was a hit first in Africa and then across Europe, and it became the first African single to sell more than a million copies. Kanté died in Conakry, the Guinean capital, in May 2020, at 70. His record had shown that the griots' harp, plugged in and set to a dance beat, could fill the dance floors of Europe.
