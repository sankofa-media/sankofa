Carmen Jones is a Hollywood musical of 1954 with an all-Black cast. It began on Broadway in 1943, when Oscar Hammerstein II rewrote Bizet's opera Carmen for Black performers, keeping the music: Carmen now worked in a parachute factory in the wartime South, and Don José became a soldier named Joe.

20th Century Fox backed the director Otto Preminger in bringing it to the screen. Dorothy Dandridge, a nightclub singer from Cleveland, wanted the title role. At her first meeting Preminger found her too poised and ladylike for it. She came back in flashy clothes and heavy make-up and won him over. Her singing voice, like Harry Belafonte's as Joe, was dubbed by opera singers; hers belonged to the mezzo-soprano Marilyn Horne.

The film was a hit. Dandridge, dressed as Carmen, appeared on the cover of Life, and she became the first Black woman nominated for the Academy Award for best actress. Yet film offers came slowly, and Bess in Porgy and Bess (1959) was her last great part as a star. She died in 1965, aged 42.

In 1992 the film was selected for the US National Film Registry.
