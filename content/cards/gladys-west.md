Gladys Mae Brown was born in 1930 in Sutherland, in rural Dinwiddie County, Virginia. Her parents worked a small farm, and as a child she helped harvest the crops. Mathematics was her way out: as valedictorian of her high school she won a full scholarship to Virginia State College, a historically Black college, and took degrees in mathematics there.

In 1956 she was hired as a mathematician at the Navy's weapons laboratory in Dahlgren, Virginia, only its fourth Black employee. There she married another Black mathematician on the base, Ira West. She moved from solving equations by hand to programming computers; an early project used the Navy's NORC computer to work out the motion of Pluto relative to Neptune. In 1978 she became project manager for Seasat, a satellite designed to study the oceans from orbit. From that work came GEOSAT and programs that let a computer allow for gravity, tides and other forces, calculate satellite orbits precisely and build a model of the Earth's true shape, the geoid. That model and its later versions let GPS fix any point on Earth accurately.

She retired in 1998 and earned a doctorate at 70. Recognition came only in 2018, from the Virginia legislature and the Air Force Space and Missile Pioneers Hall of Fame. She died in January 2026, aged 95.
