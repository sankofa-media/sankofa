The Brotherhood of the Holy Spirit of the Congos of Villa Mella is a religious confraternity in Villa Mella, on the northern edge of Santo Domingo in the Dominican Republic. Brotherhoods of the Holy Spirit go back to colonial times on the island, and UNESCO dates the founding of this one to the sixteenth century, by enslaved Africans and people of mixed descent. It joins Catholic and African traditions.

Its musicians play hand drums called congos, and the brotherhood holds that the drums came from the Holy Spirit itself, who appeared with them in Villa Mella.

Each year the brotherhood celebrates the feast of the Holy Spirit with prayers, songs and dances. Above all, it buries its own. The congos play at the funeral rites of its members: on the ninth day after a death and again, years later, at the farewell ceremony called the Banco.

In 2001 UNESCO proclaimed the brotherhood a Masterpiece of the Oral and Intangible Heritage of Humanity. UNESCO warned that fast urban growth, migration, unemployment and prejudice threaten it, and its members still complain that the state does too little to help. In Villa Mella a colonial church brotherhood became the place where the descendants of enslaved Africans kept their drums and their way of honouring the dead.
