Krio is the English-based creole of Sierra Leone. Only about one person in ten speaks it as a mother tongue, yet most of the country uses it as a common language between peoples. English remains the official language and the language of prestige.

Krio was made by people who had crossed the Atlantic in the other direction. In 1787 the British founded a settlement on the Freetown peninsula for Black people who had come from the Americas by way of England. They were joined by formerly enslaved people resettled from Nova Scotia in Canada, and by settlers from Jamaica. Then came Africans freed from slave ships once Britain had outlawed the trade, many of them from lands where Yoruba and Igbo were spoken.

So the language carries all of them. Most of its words are English, but Yoruba left a deep mark on it, and so did the creole of Jamaica. In 1980 Clifford Fyle and Eldred Jones published a Krio–English dictionary, and Krio became a language with a book of its own words. A colony founded to settle the formerly enslaved left Sierra Leone the language its peoples share.
