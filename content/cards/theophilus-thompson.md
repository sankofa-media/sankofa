In the 1870s American chess players traded problems — set positions with a task such as "mate in four" — through small journals such as the Dubuque Chess Journal in Iowa. Theophilus Augustus Thompson was born in Frederick, Maryland, on 21 April 1855, to enslaved parents, and was freed when slavery ended. At thirteen he went to work as a house servant in Carroll County, and in 1870 he came back to Frederick. In April 1872 he watched two men play chess. He did not understand the game and was afraid to annoy the players with questions, so he simply followed every move. One of them, John K. Hanshew, publisher of the Maryland Chess Review, heard how keen he was, lent him a board and men, and left him some problems to solve. Soon Thompson was composing his own and sending them to the Dubuque journal. In 1873 they came out there as a book, Chess Problems: Either to Play and Mate, or Compel Self-mate in Four Moves, sold by the journal's editor, Orestes Brownson Jr., with a short account of the author's life. A London chess magazine praised it in 1874. Almost nothing else about Thompson was recorded, and even the year of his death is uncertain. Chess historians count him among the first Black American players of note.
