In 1955 the city buses of Montgomery, Alabama, were segregated by law, and Black riders could be ordered to the back to make room for white ones. On 2 March, Claudette Colvin, a 15-year-old high-school student, was riding home when the driver told her to give up her seat to a white woman. She refused, saying she had paid her fare and it was her constitutional right. Two police officers hauled her off the bus, her schoolbooks flying, and handcuffed her. She later said she felt Harriet Tubman and Sojourner Truth pressing down on her, keeping her in her seat.

A juvenile court convicted her. On appeal the segregation charges fell, but a conviction for assaulting an officer stood, with a probation no one ever told her had ended. Nine months later Rosa Parks was arrested and the [[montgomery-bus-boycott|bus boycott]] began. In 1956 Colvin was one of the plaintiffs in Browder v. Gayle, the case in which a federal court struck down bus segregation and the Supreme Court upheld the ruling.

She moved to New York and lived there for decades. In December 2021, at her request, a judge expunged her juvenile record; she was 82. She died on 13 January 2026.
