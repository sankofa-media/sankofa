Ntozake Shange was born Paulette Williams in Trenton, New Jersey, in 1948. She studied at Barnard College and then at the University of Southern California, and in those years she took a new name: Ntozake, "she who comes with her own things", and Shange, "she who walks like a lion".

In the San Francisco Bay Area she began to improvise with a few women poets and dancers in bars and studios, and out of her poems grew a new kind of theatre. She coined a word for it, the choreopoem: poetry joined to dance and music. Seven women, each named only by the colour she wears, the lady in red, the lady in brown and the rest, tell of lovers, of racism and sexism, of violence and rape, and of surviving all of it. The piece reached New York in July 1975, moved to the Public Theater, and on 15 September 1976 opened on Broadway at the Booth Theatre.

It won an Obie Award and was nominated for a Tony. In 2010 Tyler Perry made it into a film, and in 2022 it came back to the Booth Theatre in a revival directed and choreographed by Camille A. Brown. Shange died in 2018, and the women in colours are still on stage.
