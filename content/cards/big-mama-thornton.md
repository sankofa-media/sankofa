Willie Mae "Big Mama" Thornton (1926–1984) was a blues singer from Alabama who also played drums and harmonica. As a teenager she went on the road with Sammy Green's Hot Harlem Revue; later she settled in Houston, where Don Robey signed her to his Peacock label. In August 1952 the young songwriters Jerry Leiber and Mike Stoller offered her "Hound Dog". Released by Peacock in 1953, her recording went to number one on the R&B chart, three years before Elvis Presley touched the song; how many copies it sold is disputed. In 1956 Presley's version brought him fame and a fortune. In a 1974 interview she set his millions against her own zero.

She kept singing. Her own song "Ball and Chain" became a signature for Janis Joplin, and Thornton toured Europe with the American Folk Blues Festival. She died on 25 July 1984. Forty years later, in 2024, she was inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame. Like [[rosetta-tharpe|Sister Rosetta Tharpe]], she is a dependency that [[rock-n-roll|rock and roll]] was slow to credit, and her voice comes straight out of [[blues|the blues]].
