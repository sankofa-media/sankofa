[[san-basilio-de-palenque|San Basilio de Palenque]], in the hills south-east of Cartagena de Indias, began as a refuge built by people who escaped slavery. For generations its women have come to the walled old city of Cartagena to sell: fruit and coconut sweets in a wide basin balanced on the head, colourful dresses, and a loud cry announcing what they carry.

Over time the palenquera became the emblem of Cartagena, and the city speaks of these women as its living heritage.

In March 2019 police and public-space officials, applying a new national police code against the misuse of public space, evicted Angelina Cassiani from the spot on Plaza de Bolívar where she had sold fruit for twenty years and confiscated her basin. On 26 March dozens of palenqueras marched through the historic centre carrying their basins, demanding respect and an exemption from the code.

Years later they are still there, walking the old streets with their basins and calling out their fruit.
