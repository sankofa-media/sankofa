Claudia Jones was a Trinidad-born journalist and Communist who answered race riots in London with a carnival. Born in 1915 and raised in Harlem, she became a Communist Party activist and writer in New York. She was jailed four times. In the McCarthy years she was convicted under the Smith Act, served a sentence of a year and a day in the women's prison at Alderson, West Virginia, and in 1955 the United States deported her to Britain. In March 1958 she founded the West Indian Gazette in Brixton. That summer race riots broke out in Notting Hill and Nottingham. In January 1959 she answered with a Caribbean carnival at St Pancras Town Hall, and the BBC televised part of it. She staged such indoor carnivals every year until her death in December 1964, at 49; the Gazette folded the following year. Elements of those carnivals passed into the outdoor Notting Hill Carnival, first held in 1966, which became a London institution and still runs today. She is buried in Highgate Cemetery, next to Karl Marx. Back in Trinidad, carnival had its new instrument, the [[steelpan|steelpan]]; her paper had an American elder in the [[chicago-defender|Chicago Defender]].
