Killer of Sheep is a feature film that Charles Burnett made while he was a student at the UCLA film school, shooting it in black and white on 16 mm film over spare weekends from the early 1970s and finishing it in 1977. It is set in Watts, a Black neighbourhood of South Los Angeles.

There is hardly a plot. Stan works in a slaughterhouse, and the work is wearing him down; around him are his wife, his children and the neighbourhood kids. One evening he and his wife slow-dance in their living room to Dinah Washington's "This Bitter Earth".

That soundtrack of Black music was the trap. Burnett had never meant the film for cinemas and had not cleared the rights to the songs. It won the critics' prize at the Berlin festival in 1981 and entered the Library of Congress's National Film Registry in 1990, yet for decades almost nobody could see it. UCLA's archive restored it in 2000; Milestone Films spent years clearing the music, helped by a gift from the director Steven Soderbergh, and in 2007 the film finally reached cinemas, three decades late.
