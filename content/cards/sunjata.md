The Epic of Sunjata tells how the Mali Empire began in the early thirteenth century, and griots have sung it ever since. Its hero, Sunjata Keita, is the son of Sogolon. Driven into exile with his mother by a scheming stepmother, he grew up abroad, came back at the head of an alliance of neighbouring kingdoms and defeated Sumaoro Kanté, king of Sosso, at the battle of Kirina. Sunjata founded the empire that [[mansa-musa|Mansa Musa]] would later rule.

Later scholars of the region kept their learning in books, such as [[timbuktu-manuscripts|the Timbuktu manuscripts]]; Sunjata's story lives as performance, not as a book. The jeli, or griot, trained from childhood, sings the epic to the balafon, as Yoruba drummers make [[talking-drums|their drums]] carry words. In Nyagassola, in Guinea, Kouyaté griots still keep the Sosso-Bala, a balafon said to have belonged to Sumaoro himself, and pass it down from generation to generation.
