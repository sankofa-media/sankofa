In the early seventeenth century the Portuguese, from Luanda, waged war on Ndongo, a Mbundu kingdom in today's Angola, for land and slaves. In 1622 the king sent his sister Njinga to Luanda to negotiate peace. Offered a floor mat while the Portuguese governor sat in a chair, she had an attendant kneel and sat on them, level with him.

She won a treaty and was baptised Ana de Sousa, but the wars soon resumed. After her brother's death in 1624 she took power in Ndongo, then the kingdom of Matamba, and allied with the Dutch, who held Luanda from 1641 until the Portuguese drove them out in 1648. She was also a slave trader who sold war captives to the Portuguese.

In 1656, after decades of war, she made peace with Portugal. She died in 1663, at about eighty, still a queen, and her sister succeeded her. In Angola she is a national heroine.
