In late-1960s Brixton, in south London, Black residents lived with years of police harassment and a housing crisis that hit them hardest. Olive Morris was born in Harewood, Jamaica, in 1952 and came to south London at nine to join her parents. In November 1969, at seventeen, she stepped in when police in Brixton arrested a Nigerian diplomat. Officers beat her and racially abused her; she was convicted and given a suspended prison sentence. She joined the youth section of the British Black Panther movement.

In 1973 she and her friend Liz Obi squatted an empty flat at 121 Railton Road. The squat became an organising centre for Brixton's Black community groups, and later the building housed Sabarr, a Black community bookshop. Morris was a founding member of the Brixton Black Women's Group. She studied at Manchester University, and in 1978, with Stella Dadzie and others, founded the Organisation of Women of African and Asian Descent. Back in Brixton she worked at the community law centre and campaigned against the "sus" laws, which let police arrest anyone they suspected of being about to commit a crime.

She died of non-Hodgkin's lymphoma on 12 July 1979, aged 27. Number 121 stayed a squat until 1999, and Brixton later voted to put her on its own local currency, the Brixton Pound.
