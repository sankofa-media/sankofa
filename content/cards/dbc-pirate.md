The Dread Broadcasting Corporation, or DBC, was an unlicensed radio station in London in the early 1980s, at a time when Black music had little room on British radio. [[reggae|Reggae]] got a couple of hours a week on the air at most, and Leroy Anderson, known as Lepke, wanted Black music to get more exposure.

In 1981 Lepke acquired a medium-wave transmitter and strung its aerial up in his back garden in Neasden, north-west London. DBC is remembered as Britain's first Black-owned radio station; The Wire called it the first in Europe. [[reggae|Reggae]] was its core, but the station also played [[rhythm-and-blues|R&B]], soul, [[funk|funk]] and African music, and it later moved from medium wave to FM. Its jingles invited listeners to "tune in if yuh rankin'".

When DBC fell silent, some of its DJs carried on under the name JBC. Lepke died in March 2018. Run from a back garden, DBC showed that Black Londoners could put their own music on the air, played their own way, without waiting for anyone's permission.
