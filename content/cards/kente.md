Kente is a woven cloth of what is now Ghana, and Asante tradition says two men of Bonwire learned to weave it by watching a spider spin its web. It is woven in long narrow strips on horizontal looms, then sewn edge to edge into a large cloth. Ewe weavers to the east have their own tradition. The finest, most intricate kente was once reserved for Asante royalty. Kente designs carry names, taken from proverbs, sayings or events, so a cloth speaks as well as dazzles, much as the stamped signs of [[adinkra|adinkra cloth]] do, or as [[talking-drums|talking drums]] carry words in sound. Bonwire is still the capital of kente weaving, and the cloth has travelled far beyond it. In the United States it became a sign of connection to Africa, and kente stoles became a fixture of Black graduations.
