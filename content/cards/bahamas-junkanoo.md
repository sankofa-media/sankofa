Junkanoo is the great street masquerade of Nassau, in the Bahamas. Groups of masked dancers in huge costumes of crepe paper over cardboard parade down Bay Street on Boxing Day and New Year's Day, to goatskin drums, cowbells, whistles and brass. Its old name was John Canoe.

It began among enslaved people. The days after Christmas were one of the very few times of the year when they were let off work, and they spent them in masquerade. After emancipation the parade kept its edge. On Boxing Day masqueraders from Nassau's Black neighbourhoods poured into Bay Street, the white-run business district, breaking the town's unwritten lines of segregation, and colonial officials feared a crowd they could not govern.

That fear came to a head in 1942. Black labourers building wartime airfields on New Providence rioted over their pay, and the colonial government banned all street parades, Junkanoo included. The ban lasted until 1948, when Junkanoo returned to Bay Street. Since then rival groups, each in costumes made by hand, compete every year for the top prize.

A celebration that enslaved people squeezed out of a single holiday now fills the main street of the capital twice a year.
