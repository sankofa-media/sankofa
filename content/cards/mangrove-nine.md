By the end of the 1960s the Mangrove, Frank Crichlow's Trinidadian restaurant at 8 All Saints Road in Notting Hill, west London, was the hub of the area's West Indian community. Police called it a drugs den. Between January 1969 and July 1970 they raided it twelve times and found no evidence of crime.

On 9 August 1970 about 150 people marched to local police stations to demand an end to the raids, and the march ended in clashes with the police. Nine organisers were charged with incitement to riot, among them Crichlow, Darcus Howe and Altheia Jones-LeCointe, leader of the British Black Panthers. At the Old Bailey in 1971 Howe and Jones-LeCointe defended themselves and demanded an all-Black jury, a jury of their peers. They did not get one, but by challenging juror after juror the defence ensured that two of the twelve were Black.

After a 55-day trial all nine were acquitted of incitement to riot, and five were cleared of every charge. Summing up, Judge Edward Clarke said the trial had "regrettably shown evidence of racial hatred on both sides". It was the first time a British judge had acknowledged racial prejudice in the Metropolitan Police.
