Buganda is an old kingdom in what is now Uganda, ruled by kings called kabakas. In 1882 Kabaka Mutesa I built his palace on Kasubi Hill, in what is now Kampala. When he died in 1884 he was buried inside it, breaking with the custom that each king lay in a place of his own. Three successors joined him: Mwanga II, Daudi Chwa II and Mutesa II.

Their resting place is the Muzibu Azaala Mpanga, a round house under a great thatched dome, raised entirely from plant materials: wooden poles, grass, reeds and wattle. Inside, a curtain of bark cloth hides the "forest" where the four kings lie, and only a few could pass beyond it, the widows of the kabakas among them. The site became a World Heritage site in 2001.

On the evening of 16 March 2010 fire destroyed the main house. UNESCO put Kasubi on its danger list, and the Baganda set out to rebuild with the same poles, grass and bark cloth, passing the old building skills to a younger generation. The work was finished in the summer of 2023, along with a fire-fighting system and trained volunteer firefighters, and on 12 September 2023 the site came off the danger list. It is not a ruin but a living shrine, where the kingdom's rituals go on.
