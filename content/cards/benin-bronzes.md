The Benin Bronzes are the brass plaques and other treasures made for the court of the Oba, the king of Benin, a kingdom in what is now Edo State in southern Nigeria, not the modern Republic of Benin. The Oba's brass casters, the royal guild of Igun Eronmwon, worked in lost wax: a detailed wax model over a clay core, clay over the wax, the wax melted out and molten metal poured in. When the Portuguese arrived, the casters made them a subject too, putting the newcomers onto the palace plaques as foreign merchants.

In the winter of 1896–97 a party of British officials and traders on its way to Benin City was ambushed, and several of its members were killed. In February 1897 a British punitive expedition took the city, deposed and exiled Oba Ovonramwen, and carried thousands of objects off to Europe. In 2022 London's Horniman Museum transferred ownership of 72 objects to Nigeria, and in December 2022 Germany's foreign minister handed 20 back in Abuja, the first of more than 1,000 Germany pledged to return. The plaques are a record of the Benin court and of its first European visitors, set down by the court's own artists; [[kente|kente cloth]], [[adinkra|adinkra signs]] and [[nsibidi|Nsibidi]] are other West African arts that carry meaning in cloth and sign.
