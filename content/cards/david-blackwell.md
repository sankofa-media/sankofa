David Blackwell was born in 1919 in Centralia, Illinois, the son of a railway worker. In 1941, at 22, he finished a doctorate on Markov chains at the University of Illinois and won a Rosenwald fellowship to the Institute for Advanced Study in Princeton. Fellows of the Institute usually enjoyed the hospitality of Princeton University next door, but the university had never had a Black student, and because of his race it denied Blackwell what others were given.

Looking for a job, he wrote to more than a hundred Black colleges. After posts at Southern University in Baton Rouge and Clark College in Atlanta, he joined Howard University in Washington in 1944, and by 1947 he headed its mathematics department.

His name is attached to results across probability, statistics and game theory; the best known is the Rao-Blackwell theorem, a way of improving statistical estimates. In 1954 he moved to the University of California, Berkeley, where he became the first Black tenured professor. In 1965 he became the first African American member of the National Academy of Sciences. He died in Berkeley in 2010.
