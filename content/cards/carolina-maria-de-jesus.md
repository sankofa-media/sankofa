Carolina Maria de Jesus was a Brazilian writer who lived by collecting waste paper. Born in Sacramento, Minas Gerais, she had two years of school; in São Paulo she built a shack in the Canindé favela, raised three children there, and kept writing. In 1958 the reporter Audálio Dantas saw her notebooks and printed excerpts. In 1960 her diary came out as Quarto de Despejo, "Room of Garbage", and sold tens of thousands of copies within months. It was eventually translated into 13 languages, and as Child of the Dark it reached readers in English. How much Dantas edited it is contested.

With the money she moved her family into a brick house outside the favela, but fame did not last. Her later books, some published at her own expense, found few readers; by the late 1960s she was close to poverty again and withdrew to a small farm at Parelheiros, on the edge of São Paulo. She died in 1977 on the verge of destitution. Her diary gave the people of the favela a voice of their own in Brazilian literature. Like [[hurston|Zora Neale Hurston]] and [[jacobs-garret|Harriet Jacobs, hidden in her garret]], she wrote from a place literature rarely looked, in a country where [[what-black-means|what "Black" means]] is drawn differently than in the United States.
