In the 1870s football was a young game whose rules were still being settled. Andrew Watson was born in Georgetown, Demerara, in British Guiana, in 1856 or 1857, the son of Peter Miller, a Scottish sugar planter, and a local woman, Rose Watson. Sent to Britain as a boy, he went to school in Halifax and London, and in 1875 began studying natural philosophy, mathematics and engineering at Glasgow University.

In Glasgow he took up football. A composed full back, he joined Queen's Park, the great club of the day, and won three Scottish Cups. On 12 March 1881 he captained Scotland against England at the Kennington Oval, and Scotland won 6–1. He played twice more for his country and won both times. In England he was invited into the Corinthians, an exclusive amateur club founded to lift the standard of English football.

Later he trained as a marine engineer and went to sea; he died in 1921. For more than a century his achievement went unrecognised. Only in recent decades have researchers restored him to history as the world's first Black international footballer.
