Natchitoches, on the Red River in Louisiana, was a French colonial post founded by Louis Juchereau de St. Denis. Around 1742 a girl later known as Marie Thérèse Coincoin was born into slavery in his household.

As a young woman she was leased out to Claude Thomas Pierre Metoyer, a French-born merchant. Their relationship lasted some twenty years and produced ten children. Metoyer bought her and set her free. On her own land at Cedar Bend she grew tobacco, and in 1792 she sent a cargo of it down the river to New Orleans.

Her family rose fast. Her son Louis Metoyer developed the plantation later called Melrose, long credited by legend to his mother. Before the Civil War her descendants, the Metoyers, were among the wealthiest families of African descent in the South. Freedom had a hard edge here too: on her farm Coincoin came to own some sixteen enslaved people.

She died around 1816. Her descendants still live along the Cane River, and her life shows how a woman born enslaved could found a free dynasty within the same system of slavery that had owned her.
