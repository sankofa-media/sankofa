In the eighteenth century the gold of Minas Gerais filled Brazil's mining towns with churches, and the most celebrated sculptor and architect among their builders was Antônio Francisco Lisboa. He was born in Vila Rica, today's Ouro Preto, probably in 1738, the son of the Portuguese architect Manuel Francisco Lisboa and an African woman.

A disease, perhaps leprosy, slowly deformed his limbs and gave him his nickname, Aleijadinho, "the little cripple". He lost the use of his hands, but he kept working with his tools strapped to his arms. His last great commission was the pilgrimage sanctuary of Bom Jesus de Matosinhos in Congonhas. In the late 1790s he made the life-size cedar figures of the Passion for the chapels on the path up the hill. Then, between 1800 and 1805, he carved in soapstone the twelve Old Testament prophets that stand on the parapets of the church stairway.

He died in 1814. In 1985 the sanctuary at Congonhas was inscribed on UNESCO's World Heritage List, and its prophets are counted among the summits of the Brazilian Baroque.
