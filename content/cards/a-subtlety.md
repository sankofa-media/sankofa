The Domino Sugar factory in Williamsburg, Brooklyn, stopped refining sugar in 2004. Ten years later it stood empty, awaiting demolition. The New York arts organisation Creative Time asked Kara Walker to make her first large public work inside it.

She called it, at full length, A Subtlety, or the Marvelous Sugar Baby, an Homage to the unpaid and overworked Artisans who have refined our Sweet tastes from the cane fields to the Kitchens of the New World. A foam body some 75 feet long and 35 feet high was coated in tons of sugar: a sphinx with the kerchiefed head of a mammy figure, gleaming white. Around her stood statues of Black boys carrying baskets of fruit.

Crowds came, and many took joking selfies; Walker had a camera crew film them. When the show closed, the sphinx was broken up. Walker kept one piece, the left hand, and the film of its visitors became a work of its own. For a few weeks the labour that sugar was made of stood, in sugar, in the place where it had been refined.
