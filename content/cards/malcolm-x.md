Malcolm X was born Malcolm Little in Omaha, Nebraska, on 19 May 1925. His father died when he was six, killed, the family believed, by white supremacists. In 1946 Malcolm was sent to prison in Massachusetts for burglary, and there he joined the Nation of Islam. Released in 1952, he gave up his surname as a "slave name" and took an X. By the late 1950s he was the movement's leading spokesman, and his views stood in sharp contrast to the nonviolence of [[martin-luther-king|Martin Luther King Jr.]]

On 26 March 1964 the two men met face to face in a corridor of the US Capitol, after King's press conference on the Senate debate over the [[civil-rights-act-1964|Civil Rights Act]]; a news photographer caught them side by side. Earlier that month Malcolm had broken with the Nation of Islam. He made the pilgrimage to Mecca, travelled through Africa and founded the Organization of Afro-American Unity, setting Black America's struggle inside a worldwide one.

On 21 February 1965 he was shot dead at the Audubon Ballroom in upper Manhattan. In November 2021 a Manhattan judge vacated the murder convictions of two of the men jailed for the killing, Muhammad Aziz and Khalil Islam. In his last eleven months he had grown from one movement's minister into a voice heard from Harlem to Accra.
