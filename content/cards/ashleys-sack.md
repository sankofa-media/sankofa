Ashley's Sack is a cotton sack from South Carolina embroidered with the story of an enslaved mother and daughter. When nine-year-old Ashley was sold in the 1850s, her mother Rose gave her the sack with a tattered dress, three handfuls of pecans and a braid of Rose's hair. They never saw each other again. Generations later, Ashley's granddaughter, Ruth Middleton, who lived in Philadelphia, embroidered that story onto the sack and signed it "Ruth Middleton 1921". Mark Auslander traced a Rose and an Ashley to the Charleston estate of Robert Martin, who died in 1852, a reconstruction rather than proof. Much later, a shopper at a Tennessee flea market found the sack in a bin of old rags, read the stitching and bought the whole bin for $20. It now belongs to Middleton Place, near Charleston, and has been shown at the Smithsonian's African American history museum. In 2021 the historian Tiya Miles won the National Book Award for nonfiction with All That She Carried, a book about the sack. Other traces of enslaved lives survive in [[dave-the-potter|the jars of Dave the Potter]] and [[wpa-narratives|the WPA slave narratives]], and the rule that bondage passed from mother to child was written into [[partus-1662|Virginia's 1662 law of partus sequitur ventrem]].
