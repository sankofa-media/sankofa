Dona Beatriz Kimpa Vita was born around 1684 into the nobility of Kongo, a kingdom that had been Christian since its king was baptised in 1491. Since 1665 rival branches of the royal family had been fighting over the throne, and the old capital, São Salvador, stood abandoned. As a young woman she worked as a nganga, a mediator with the spirits.

In 1703 or 1704, gravely ill, she said Saint Anthony of Padua had entered her and charged her with restoring Kongo. She taught that Jesus and Mary had been born in Kongo, in the region of São Salvador, and that the founders of the faith were Black, and she turned against the Italian Capuchin missionaries. She moved into the ruins of the capital, and thousands of people came to settle there with her, calling for one king to reunite the country.

Her movement became part of the war of factions. She was captured and handed over to King Pedro IV, condemned as a heretic and burned on 2 July 1706, aged about 22. The movement outlived her: Pedro IV took São Salvador in 1709 and restored the kingdom. Today churches in Angola and Congo, among them Bundu dia Kongo, claim her as their founder, and her story runs back to the Christian Kongo of [[afonso-kongo-letters|Afonso I]].
