The scholar who named misogynoir, misogyny aimed at Black women in particular, learned to fight it as a student. In 2004 Spelman College students led by Moya Bailey and Asha Jennings protested Nelly's "Tip Drill", a video so explicit that BET aired it only on its late-night show Uncut. A bone-marrow drive tied to his foundation was cancelled, and who cancelled it is disputed. In 2010 Bailey named what they had fought, and the word is now in dictionaries. The sharpest critics of misogyny in [[hip-hop|hip-hop]] came from inside it. In 1991 Dr. Dre assaulted the journalist Dee Barnes; he pleaded no contest, settled her lawsuit out of court and in 2015 publicly apologised to the women he had hurt. Queen Latifah's "U.N.I.T.Y." (1993) took on misogyny and violence and won a Grammy in 1995.
