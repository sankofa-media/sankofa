"Rapper's Delight" is a 1979 single by the Sugarhill Gang, the record that carried [[hip-hop]] from the parties and street corners of New York onto the American pop charts. It was the idea of Sylvia Robinson, a record-business veteran who, with her husband Joe, faced bankruptcy as their label All Platinum collapsed. They reorganised it as Sugar Hill Records, based in Englewood, New Jersey, and named after a Harlem neighbourhood.

Robinson put together a group of three unknowns: Wonder Mike, Master Gee and Big Bank Hank, whom she found working at a pizza parlour in Englewood. They recorded the song in a single take over the bass line of Chic's disco hit "Good Times". Some of Hank's rhymes had been written by the Bronx rapper Grandmaster Caz, whose name was left off the credits.

It was not the first rap record, but it was the first to break into the pop Top 40, reaching number 36 on the Billboard Hot 100. Chic's Nile Rodgers and Bernard Edwards threatened to sue and won a songwriting credit. The single went on to sell an estimated 14 million copies, and in 2011 the Library of Congress added it to the National Recording Registry.
