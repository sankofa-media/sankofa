In mid-nineteenth-century America most medical schools refused Black students, men and women alike. Rebecca Davis was born in Delaware in 1831 and raised by an aunt in Pennsylvania whose neighbours kept calling on her to nurse their sick; watching her, the girl wrote later, she early wished to relieve the sufferings of others. From 1852 to 1860 she worked as a nurse for doctors in Charlestown, Massachusetts, without formal training, since the first American nursing school opened only in 1873. In 1860 she was admitted to the New England Female Medical College in Boston. Her first husband, Wyatt Lee, died of tuberculosis in 1863, and on 1 March 1864 she graduated a "doctress of medicine", the first African American woman in the United States to earn an MD. After the war she went to Richmond, Virginia, and worked with the Freedmen's Bureau and missionary groups, treating formerly enslaved people who had no other doctor. Back in Boston she practised from her home on Joy Street, Beacon Hill, seeing patients whether or not they could pay. In 1883 she published A Book of Medical Discourses, advice on the health of women and children and one of the first medical books by an African American author. She died in 1895, and no portrait of her survives.
