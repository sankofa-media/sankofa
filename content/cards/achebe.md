Chinua Achebe was a Nigerian novelist, born in 1930 in Ogidi in eastern Nigeria, whose first novel nearly disappeared in the post. Working in broadcasting in Lagos, he wrote out of indignation at Joyce Cary's Mister Johnson, to tell an Igbo village's story from the inside.

He mailed the handwritten manuscript of Things Fall Apart to a London typing agency, and the agency went silent. Angela Beattie, his broadcasting supervisor, visited it while on leave and demanded that the typed manuscript be posted to Lagos. Heinemann printed 2,000 copies in 1958, under a title taken from Yeats's "The Second Coming". The book went on to sell more than ten million copies and to be translated into some 50 languages. In 1962 Heinemann built the African Writers Series around it, with Achebe as editorial adviser for ten years; it stands between [[tutuola|Amos Tutuola's The Palm-Wine Drinkard]] before it and [[ngugi|Ngũgĩ wa Thiong'o]] after.

In 1975 at Amherst he called Conrad's Heart of Darkness racist, in a lecture later printed as "An Image of Africa". A car accident in Nigeria in 1990 left him paralysed. He taught at Bard College and from 2009 at Brown, and died in Boston on 21 March 2013.
