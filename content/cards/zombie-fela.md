"Zombie" is an [[afrobeat|afrobeat]] track that Fela Kuti released with his band Africa 70 in 1976, when Nigeria was ruled by the military. Over a driving groove and blaring horns, its zombie, who turns, stops and thinks only when told to, is a soldier of the Nigerian army.

The record became a huge hit across the country. Fela lived with his family, musicians and followers in a Lagos compound he called the Kalakuta Republic, and the police had raided it before. On 18 February 1977 about a thousand soldiers stormed it, beat Fela and burned the compound to the ground. They threw his mother, Funmilayo Ransome-Kuti, from a second-floor window. She died of her injuries in April 1978.

Fela kept singing. In 1978 Ghana expelled him. After her death he and his supporters carried a symbolic coffin to Dodan Barracks, the headquarters of the head of state, General Obasanjo, as a gesture of defiance. A song that called soldiers zombies had cost him his home and his mother, and he answered with more songs.
