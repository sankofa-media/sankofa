L'Étudiant noir, "The Black Student", was a small paper published in Paris in 1935 by students from France's colonies. It grew out of L'Étudiant martiniquais, the bulletin of the Association of Martinican Students. Under Aimé Césaire, who became the association's president in 1934, the paper changed its name and its horizon: it now spoke for Black students from the Caribbean and from Africa together.

Its circle was a group of friends. Césaire and Léon-Gontran Damas had been classmates at the Lycée Schoelcher in Martinique; Césaire had met the Senegalese Léopold Sédar Senghor in 1931 at the Lycée Louis-le-Grand in Paris. Birago Diop and Ousmane Socé wrote for it too. The first issue came out in March 1935. In the third, in May–June, Césaire's article "Racial consciousness and social revolution" used the word négritude.

The word outlived the paper. Négritude grew into a movement of poets, building on what [[nardal-sisters|the Nardal sisters]] and [[legitime-defense|Légitime Défense]] had begun. In 1946–48 Césaire, Senghor and Damas all sat in the French parliament, and Césaire was mayor of Fort-de-France.
