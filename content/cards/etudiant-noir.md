L'Étudiant noir, "The Black Student", was a small paper published in Paris in 1935 by students from France's colonies. It grew out of L'Étudiant martiniquais, the bulletin of the Association of Martinican Students. With the new name came a new horizon: the paper now spoke for Black students from the Caribbean and from Africa together, and the young Martinican Aimé Césaire was at its heart.

Its circle was a group of friends. Césaire and Léon-Gontran Damas had been classmates at the Lycée Schoelcher in Martinique; in Paris, at the Lycée Louis-le-Grand, Césaire had befriended the Senegalese Léopold Sédar Senghor. Birago Diop and Ousmane Socé wrote for it too. The first issue came out in March 1935. In the third, in May–June, Césaire's article "Racial consciousness and social revolution" used the word négritude.

The word outlived the paper. Négritude grew into a movement of poets, building on what [[nardal-sisters|the Nardal sisters]] and [[legitime-defense|Légitime Défense]] had begun. After the Second World War Césaire, Senghor and Damas all sat in the French National Assembly, and Césaire was also mayor of Fort-de-France.
