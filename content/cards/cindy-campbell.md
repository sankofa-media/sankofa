[[hip-hop|Hip-hop]]'s famous birthday party was a high-school girl's fundraiser. On 11 August 1973 Cindy Campbell held a party in the recreation room of her family's building, 1520 Sedgwick Avenue in the Bronx, because she needed money for new clothes for the school year. She wrote the invitations on index cards and asked her brother Clive to play the music.

He was DJ Kool Herc, born in Kingston and raised inside its sound-system culture ([[reggae|reggae]]). The room was packed. Herc had noticed that dancers waited for the short drum breaks in [[funk|funk]] and soul records, so he played two copies of the same record and switched between them to stretch the break; he called the trick the merry-go-round. Within months the parties had outgrown the room and moved outdoors, to Bronx parks such as Cedar Park.

The night is commonly called the birth of [[hip-hop|hip-hop]], though no genre starts at one party. Herc's stretched breaks, later called breakbeats, became one of the foundations of the music. And the start everyone remembers was a young woman's idea, in a culture later often criticized, not least by its own artists, for [[misogyny-in-hip-hop|misogyny]].
