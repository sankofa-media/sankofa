"Tutti Frutti" is the 1955 record that announced [[rock-n-roll|rock and roll]] with a single shout: "A wop bop a loo bop, a lop bam boom!" Little Richard, born Richard Penniman in Macon, Georgia, had been making records since the early 1950s without much notice. On 14 September 1955 he was at J&M Studio in New Orleans for Specialty Records, with the producer Robert "Bumps" Blackwell, and the session was going nowhere.

During a break he pounded out a number from his club act, an openly obscene song about "good booty". Blackwell heard a hit and brought in a local songwriter, Dorothy LaBostrie, to rewrite the words for the radio. The shout stayed; by one of Richard's accounts, he had made it up while washing dishes in Macon.

The record crossed from the rhythm-and-blues market onto the pop chart, where it reached No. 17. But the white crooner Pat Boone recorded a cover with even tamer lyrics, and his version climbed to No. 12. In the 1950s white singers routinely re-recorded Black hits for white radio, and "Tutti Frutti" became a textbook case of how segregated the music business still was. Elvis Presley covered it too. The original outlived them all, and the shout became one of the founding sounds of rock and roll.
