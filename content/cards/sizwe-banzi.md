Under apartheid, the pass laws decided where a Black South African could live and work, and a stamp in his passbook could take both away. In Port Elizabeth two actors of the Serpent Players company, John Kani and Winston Ntshona, worked with the playwright Athol Fugard, and in 1972 the three of them devised a play about those laws: Sizwe Banzi Is Dead.

Its hero has the wrong stamp in his book. He is ordered to leave the district and cannot get work. His way out is to take a dead man's identity card, give up his own name and live on under someone else's, a man officially dead so that he can earn a living.

In 1974 the play reached Broadway, where it ran at the Edison Theatre, and Kani and Ntshona both won Tony Awards for their performances. When they came home in 1976 and took it on tour in South Africa, they were arrested and detained; protests won their release. They kept playing the roles for more than three decades, and in 2008 they were still performing them together, at the Brooklyn Academy of Music in New York.
