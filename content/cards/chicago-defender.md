The Chicago Defender was a Black weekly newspaper that, during the First World War, called African Americans out of the segregated South. Robert S. Abbott, a Georgia-born lawyer, founded it in Chicago in 1905. The Defender urged Black Southerners to move north and printed the jobs waiting for them there.

In early 1917 it named 15 May as the start of the "Great Northern Drive". Black Pullman porters hid bundles on southbound trains, and some Southern towns tried to ban the paper, but it got through: by 1918 it sold 125,000 copies.

Abbott died in 1940 and left the Defender to his nephew, John Sengstacke. In print it outlived its founder by almost eighty years; the last paper edition came out on 10 July 2019, and the Defender went on online. Historians count it among the forces behind the Great Migration of Black Southerners to Northern cities. Its ancestor is [[freedoms-journal|Freedom's Journal]], its London cousin the West Indian Gazette of [[claudia-jones|Claudia Jones]], and the [[blues|blues]] took the same road north.
