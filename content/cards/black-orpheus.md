Black Orpheus, a 1959 film, moves the Greek myth of Orpheus and Eurydice to Rio de Janeiro at Carnival. It grew out of Orfeu da Conceição, a play by the Brazilian poet Vinicius de Moraes, and was directed by the Frenchman Marcel Camus. Orfeu is a tram driver on a hillside favela; he falls for the doomed Eurydice, played by Marpessa Dawn, and, like Orpheus, follows her into the world of the dead.

Almost the whole cast was Black. Orfeu was played by Breno Mello, a footballer from Porto Alegre, not an actor. The songs, by Antônio Carlos Jobim and Luiz Bonfá, among them "Manhã de Carnaval" and "A Felicidade", carried the new bossa nova to audiences around the world.

The film won the Palme d'Or at Cannes in 1959 and, entered as a French production, the Oscar for best foreign-language film. Audiences loved it. Many Brazilian critics did not: they saw a foreigner's postcard of their country, all beaches, Carnival and colourful, cheerful poverty.

Breno Mello died in his home town in 2008. The film he made with Camus is remembered above all for its music, the soundtrack that first made bossa nova famous abroad.
