Basra, Iraq's port on the Gulf, is home to a community of Iraqis of African descent. Their ancestors came from East Africa over many centuries, as sailors or as enslaved people. Nobody has counted them officially, and estimates run from about 400,000 to two million; most live in Basra province, many in the district of Zubair.

Basra knows them best through music. When a big wedding comes, the drummers are often Black men from Zubair. Among themselves they keep older rites: ceremonies of African origin, held in Zubair, that call on spirits from across the sea. Yet many have long been confined to menial jobs or to work as musicians and dancers, and they speak of discrimination because of the colour of their skin.

Barack Obama's victory in 2008 sparked an awakening. Jalal Dhiab, an activist who had founded the Free Iraqi Movement, asked for Black Iraqis to be recognised as a minority whose rights are protected, and revived the memory of [[zanj-rebellion|the Zanj revolt]] as his people's own history. In April 2013 he was assassinated in Basra.

His movement made a community long seen only at other people's celebrations visible as citizens with a history and demands of their own.
