Mariana Grajales was a free woman of colour from Santiago de Cuba, the daughter of parents who had come from Santo Domingo. In her lifetime Cuba was Spain's richest colony, a sugar island worked by enslaved Africans, where free Black families lived under constant suspicion. Her birth year is disputed: 1815 in Cuban records, 1808 in other accounts.

She raised thirteen children, four from her first marriage and nine with Marcos Maceo, on farms outside Santiago; the eldest Maceo son, Antonio, was born in 1845. When the war for independence from Spain broke out in 1868, she had the men of the family kneel before a crucifix and swear to free Cuba or die for it. Marcos was killed in 1869, and several of her sons fell. She followed the rebel camps into the mountains and nursed the wounded. José Martí later wrote that any fighter who trembled before the enemy saw Maceo's mother in her kerchief and stopped trembling.

After the war she went into exile in Kingston, Jamaica, in 1878 and died there in 1893. In 1923 her remains were brought home to Santa Ifigenia cemetery in Santiago, and in 1957 she was officially declared "Mother of Cuba". Her son Antonio became the most celebrated general of Cuba's wars of independence.
