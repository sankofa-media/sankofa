Taytu Betul was born into an aristocratic Ethiopian family in the middle of the 19th century. She was church-educated and could read and write. After four earlier marriages she married Menelik, then king of Shewa, and when he became emperor in 1889 she became empress beside him. Together they enlarged the empire, through alliances and also by conquest, above all of the Oromo lands.

Taytu chose the site of a new town and gave it its name, Addis Ababa; it became the capital of Ethiopia. Then came Italy. The Treaty of Wuchale turned out to contain an article by which Italy meant to make Ethiopia its protectorate, and Taytu fought it at court and urged war. When war came she led her own force of some five thousand soldiers, and her plan to cut the Italians off from their water helped win the campaign that ended in the Ethiopian victory over the Italian colonial army at Adwa in 1896.

In Menelik's last years she ran much of the government herself, until rivals at court, who resented her power, forced her out. She lived her last years away from the palace and died in 1917 or 1918.
