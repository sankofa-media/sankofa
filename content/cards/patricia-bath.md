Patricia Bath was born in Harlem, New York, in 1942. Her father, an immigrant from Trinidad, was the first Black motorman on the city's subway. As a young doctor at Harlem Hospital and Columbia University in the late 1960s, she saw that about half the patients in Harlem's eye clinic were visually impaired or blind, while at Columbia's clinic only a few were. Her study found blindness among Black patients at roughly double the white rate, largely for lack of eye care. Her answer was a new discipline she called community ophthalmology: preventing blindness through education, public health outreach and local medical services.

In 1975 she moved to Los Angeles and became the first woman on the faculty of UCLA's Jules Stein Eye Institute; in 1983 she became the first woman in the United States to chair an ophthalmology residency programme. In 1981 she conceived the Laserphaco Probe. A fine line, about a millimetre thick, slips through a tiny incision and uses laser light to break up the clouded lens, which is then removed and replaced. Her patent was granted on 17 May 1988, the first ever issued to a Black woman physician for a medical invention.

She died in 2019. Both her probe and her community eye care began with what she had seen in a Harlem clinic.
