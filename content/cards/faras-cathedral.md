Faras, the ancient Pachoras, stood on the Nile near today's border between Sudan and Egypt. It was a leading town of Nobadia, a Nubian kingdom whose rulers became Christian in the 6th century. Early in the 8th century a cathedral was built there, and from then until the 14th century its walls were painted with saints, archangels and Nubia's own bishops.

In the 1960s the Aswan High Dam was about to flood the valley, and UNESCO appealed for the rescue of Nubia's monuments. A Polish mission led by Kazimierz Michałowski dug at Faras from 1961 to 1964 and uncovered the cathedral. Restorers then took more than a hundred paintings off its walls, among them a portrait of Bishop Petros with Saint Peter, painted between 974 and 997.

Sudan and Poland divided the finds between them. Sixty-seven paintings went to the National Museum in Warsaw, and the rest to the Sudan National Museum in Khartoum. Faras itself went under the reservoir.

The Warsaw Faras Gallery holds Europe's largest collection of medieval Nubian art. Its Nubian bishops, painted in an African cathedral a thousand years ago, now look out from a wall in Warsaw.
