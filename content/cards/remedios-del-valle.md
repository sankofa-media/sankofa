María Remedios del Valle was a Black woman born in Buenos Aires, then a Spanish colonial city. In 1810, as the revolt against Spanish rule began, she followed the Army of the North with her husband and two sons, one of them adopted.

She tended the wounded and went into battle: at Tucumán and Salta, and in the defeats at Vilcapugio and Ayohuma in 1813. The army's commander, Manuel Belgrano, gave her the rank of captain. Taken prisoner after Ayohuma, she helped patriot prisoners escape and was flogged in public for nine days. She broke free and returned to the army. The war took her husband and both sons.

Back in Buenos Aires she lived by begging, until General Juan José Viamonte, who had known her on campaign, spoke for her. She was granted a captain's pay, then the rank of sergeant major of cavalry, and took the name Remedios Rosas in gratitude to the governor, Juan Manuel de Rosas. An army register of 8 November 1847 notes her death.

By Law 26,852, Argentina now keeps 8 November as the National Day of Afro-Argentines in her honour, and her portrait appears on banknotes beside Belgrano's.
