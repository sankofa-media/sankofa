Francia Márquez was born in 1981 in Yolombó, in the municipality of Suárez in Colombia's Cauca mountains. The Afro-Colombian families of the area have farmed and mined gold by hand for generations. She became an activist at 13, when a dam project threatened her community. Pregnant at 16, she left school and supported her child by small-scale gold mining and later by working as a maid.

Years later illegal gold miners moved onto the Ovejas River near La Toma with heavy machinery and poisoned it with mercury. Márquez organised the women of La Toma. In November 2014, 80 of them set out on a ten-day march to Bogotá, and once there they kept up their protest in the streets for 22 days. The government agreed to act, and all the illegal miners and their machines were removed from La Toma. Death threats forced Márquez to leave the region.

She went on to earn a law degree, and in 2018 she won the Goldman Environmental Prize. In 2022, as Gustavo Petro's running mate, she was elected Colombia's first Black vice-president.
