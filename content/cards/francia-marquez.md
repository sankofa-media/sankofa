Francia Márquez was born in 1981 in Yolombó, in the municipality of Suárez in Colombia's Cauca mountains. Afro-Colombians there descend from Africans brought in slavery to work the colonial gold mines, and for generations they have farmed and panned for gold along the Ovejas River. She became an activist at 13, when a dam project threatened her community. Pregnant at 16, she left school and supported her child by small-scale gold mining and later by working as a maid.

In 2014 illegal miners brought backhoes to the Ovejas near La Toma, dug open pits on the banks and poisoned the river with mercury. Márquez organised the women of La Toma. In November 80 of them set out on a ten-day march to Bogotá, and once there they kept up their protest in the streets for 22 days. The government agreed to act, and all the illegal miners and their machines were removed from La Toma. Threats forced Márquez to leave her home for Cali.

She went on to earn a law degree, and in 2018 she won the Goldman Environmental Prize. In 2022, as Gustavo Petro's running mate, she was elected Colombia's first Black vice-president.
