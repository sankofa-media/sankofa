Medefaidrin is a language with an alphabet of its own, used in worship by the Oberi Okaime, an independent church of Ibibio Christians in south-eastern Nigeria, near Itu on the Cross River. Its members speak Ibibio in daily life; Medefaidrin belongs to prayer and teaching. Its numbers run in twenties: forty is two twenties, a hundred is five.

The church grew out of the Spirit Movement of 1927, a revival that swept the Ibibio villages; its members remember it as the year the Holy Spirit came down on them. According to the community, the language was then revealed to one of its leaders, Michael Ukpong, and the church's secretary, Akpan Udofia, wrote it down. Other accounts date the revelation to the 1930s, when the two men spent years in seclusion perfecting the language and its script. The church took the name Oberi Okaime, "the church freely given".

Over the decades the language fell out of use, but by 1986 it was being taught again in Sunday school, and scholars noted a revival of interest. Today it remains the language in which the church prays. Like [[nsibidi|Nsibidi]], the older sign writing of the Cross River region, it is a script made in Africa for an African community.
