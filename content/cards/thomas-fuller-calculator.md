In eighteenth-century America a common defence of slavery held that Africans were mentally inferior to Europeans. Thomas Fuller was born in West Africa around 1710, and at about fourteen he was kidnapped and sold into slavery in Virginia. He ended up on a farm near Alexandria owned by the Cox family, stayed there for the rest of his life, and never learned to read or write. When he was about seventy, two visitors from Pennsylvania came to test the arithmetic everyone talked about. How many seconds are there in a year and a half? He answered 47,304,000. How many seconds has a man lived who is 70 years, 17 days and 12 hours old? He answered 2,210,500,800, in under two minutes and without pen or paper. One visitor, working the sum out in writing, said the number was too large. Fuller reminded him of the leap years, and with them the answer was right. Antislavery campaigners retold the scene as proof that Africans were not mentally inferior. Fuller died in 1790 at about eighty, still held on the Cox farm. Whether he taught himself to reckon in Virginia or brought a West African tradition of mental calculation with him is still debated.
