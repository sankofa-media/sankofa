In early nineteenth-century Philadelphia, Black Methodists led by Richard Allen built their own church around his Bethel congregation: the African Methodist Episcopal (AME) Church. Jarena Lee was born free in Cape May, New Jersey, on 11 February 1783, and at seven was sent to live as a servant with a white family. Later, in Philadelphia, she heard Allen preach at Bethel and was converted. When she told him that God had called her to preach, Allen replied that the church's rules made no provision for women preachers. In 1811 she married Joseph Lee, a pastor, who died six or seven years later. Years after her first request, she sat in Bethel while a visiting preacher faltered in his sermon. Lee sprang to her feet and preached on his text. Then she sat down, frightened that she would be expelled. Instead Allen rose and told the congregation that she had asked him long before, and that he now believed she was truly called to preach. She became the first woman preacher of the AME Church and travelled widely; in one year, by her own count, she covered 2,325 miles and preached 178 sermons. In 1836 she published her life story, expanded in 1849 into a journal, and she joined the American Anti-Slavery Society.
