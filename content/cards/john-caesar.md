In January 1788 the First Fleet arrived from Britain to found a penal colony at Sydney Cove. Among its convicts was John Caesar, a man of African parentage who may have been born on Madagascar. In England he had been convicted of theft and sentenced to seven years' transportation.

The settlement was a prison without walls, and Caesar kept leaving it. In December 1789 he stole a canoe and a gun and slipped into the bush; a month later he gave himself up. In 1790 he was shipped off to Norfolk Island. Back in Sydney he ran again. Late in 1795, in a clash with Aboriginal warriors, he wounded their leader Pemulwuy.

That December he escaped for the last time and lived by raiding settlers' farms. Governor John Hunter offered a reward, five gallons of spirits, for his capture. In February 1796 John Wimbow tracked him to his hideout near today's Strathfield and shot him; Caesar died a few hours later.

He is remembered as Australia's first bushranger. His story is also a reminder that people of African descent were among the convicts who founded colonial Australia.
