The "Willie Lynch speech" claims to be a talk given in 1712 on the bank of the James River in Virginia by William Lynch, a slave owner from the West Indies. He tells Virginia's planters that they can control the people they enslave by keeping them divided and setting them against one another. The text is a fake.

It has been in print since at least 1970. In 1993 Anne Taylor of the University of Missouri-St. Louis library put it on the library's Gopher server, an early internet service; she had found it in the St. Louis Black Pages, a free local paper. In October 1995 Louis Farrakhan quoted it to the Million Man March in Washington, and from there it spread far and wide, down to rap lyrics. Some versions even claim that Lynch gave his name to the word "lynching", which is also untrue.

Historians took it apart. Roy Rosenzweig wrote that its internal evidence readily betrays its twentieth-century origins, and William Jelani Cobb showed its language to be anachronistic for 1712, calling the document "absolutely fake". Its pull is plain: it offers a quick and easy explanation for division. The explanation is invented.
