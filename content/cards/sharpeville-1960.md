Sharpeville is a Black township in South Africa. Under apartheid, the pass laws forced Black South Africans to carry identity documents at all times, and police could check them whenever they liked. In 1960 the Pan Africanist Congress, a recent breakaway from the ANC, called on people to leave their passes at home on 21 March, go to police stations and offer themselves for arrest.

That morning thousands marched to the Sharpeville police station, singing freedom songs and calling "Down with passes!" Armoured cars stood outside. The police opened fire without warning. Sixty-nine people were killed and more than 180 wounded, most of them shot in the back as they ran.

The government declared a state of emergency and on 8 April banned the ANC and the PAC, which went underground and formed armed wings. On 1 April the UN Security Council had already condemned the killings. The United Nations later made 21 March the International Day for the Elimination of Racial Discrimination. In 1996 Nelson Mandela signed South Africa's democratic constitution into law at Sharpeville, and in South Africa 21 March is now Human Rights Day.
