Barbara Hillary was born in New York in 1931 and raised in Harlem by her mother, a domestic worker, after her father died when she was a small child. She studied at the New School, specialised in gerontology and worked as a nurse for 55 years. She had survived breast cancer in her twenties, and surgery for lung cancer in her sixties cost her about a quarter of her breathing capacity.

After she retired she went dog-sledding in Quebec and photographed polar bears in Manitoba. On those trips she learned that no Black woman was known to have reached the North Pole, and she decided to be the first. She trained, and raised about $25,000 for the journey, mostly from donations. She flew via Norway to a camp on the drifting Arctic ice, and on 23 April 2007, at 75, she skied the last stretch to the Pole.

In 2011, at 79, she stood at the South Pole as well. The polar ice turned her towards climate change: in 2019 she travelled to Mongolia to visit nomads whose way of life it was eroding. She died in Queens that November, at 88. A woman who began exploring after a whole career in nursing had reached both ends of the Earth.
