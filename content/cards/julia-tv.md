By the late 1960s American network television had almost no Black stars at the centre of its series. The Black-led shows of the early 1950s, among them Beulah and Amos 'n' Andy, had dealt in stereotypes and had long left the air, and the parts open to Black women on screen were mostly maids and nannies. Julia, a half-hour series that NBC launched in September 1968, broke that pattern.

Diahann Carroll played Julia Baker, a nurse whose husband had been killed in Vietnam. With her small son Corey she moves into an integrated apartment building in Los Angeles and goes to work in a medical office in the aerospace industry. The show's creator, Hal Kanter, had once written for Beulah. In the late 1960s he heard NAACP leaders deplore the lack of positive Black images on screen and took the idea of Julia to NBC.

The debate around the show was heated from the start. Critics complained, among other things, that the series had no Black man at the head of the family. Julia nevertheless ran for three seasons, until 1971, and is remembered as one of the first American series to star a Black woman in a role that was not a stereotype.
