Martin Luther King Jr. was born in Atlanta on 15 January 1929 as Michael; his name was changed to Martin later. His maternal grandfather and then his father were pastors of Ebenezer Baptist Church there. After theological study in Pennsylvania and Boston he became pastor of Dexter Avenue Baptist Church in Montgomery, Alabama, in 1954.

A year later, at 26, he was elected to lead the [[montgomery-bus-boycott|Montgomery bus boycott]]. He was arrested, and his house was bombed with his family at home; no one was hurt. From 1957 he headed the Southern Christian Leadership Conference, preaching a nonviolence drawn from Christianity and from Gandhi. Jailed in Birmingham in 1963, he wrote the "[[letter-from-birmingham-jail|Letter from Birmingham Jail]]"; that August he spoke at the [[march-on-washington|March on Washington]]. In 1964, at 35, he became the youngest recipient of the Nobel Peace Prize up to that time, and he shared the money among civil rights organisations.

The campaign at [[selma-1965|Selma]], which he joined, pushed Congress to pass the [[civil-rights-act-1964|Voting Rights Act]] in 1965. In April 1968 he went to Memphis to support striking Black sanitation workers. On 4 April he was shot dead on the balcony of the Lorraine Motel. He was 39. By then he was the best-known voice of Black America and a figure known around the world.
