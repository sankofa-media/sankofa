In the 1870s Sudan still had slave markets, among them El Obeid. A girl born around 1869 in a village in Darfur was kidnapped as a child by slave traders and fed into that trade. The shock made her forget her own name; her captors called her Bakhita, Arabic for "fortunate". She was sold again and again.

In Khartoum the Italian consul Callisto Legnani bought her, and he took her with him to Italy. There she became nanny to the small daughter of the Michieli family, and when the family left for Africa on business, their daughter and Bakhita were placed with the Canossian Sisters in Venice. When Signora Michieli came back for them, Bakhita refused to leave. The question of her status was settled in her favour: she was declared free, because slavery had no standing in Italy.

She was baptised Josephine on 9 January 1890 and later joined the Canossian order. At its convent in Schio she cooked, sewed and kept the door, welcoming visitors, and the town came to love her. She died at Schio on 8 February 1947. On 1 October 2000 Pope John Paul II canonised the girl whom slave traders had named "fortunate".
