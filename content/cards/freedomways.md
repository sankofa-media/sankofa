Freedomways was a quarterly journal of politics and culture founded in New York in 1961 by a small circle of Black left-wing activists around W. E. B. Du Bois. It was the early Cold War, and several of them had been jailed, indicted or harassed for their politics.

From the first issue its managing editor, in effect its editor-in-chief, was Esther Cooper Jackson. In the 1940s she had been a leader of the Southern Negro Youth Congress; in the 1950s her husband, the Communist James E. Jackson, was prosecuted under the Smith Act. She brought that experience to the journal and kept it going for a quarter of a century, gathering and sharpening the work of others.

Its contributors read like a roll call: James Baldwin, Lorraine Hansberry, Alice Walker, Paul Robeson, Du Bois himself, the artist Elizabeth Catlett, and from Africa, Tanzania's leader Julius Nyerere. The movement in the American South and the liberation of Africa appeared in the same pages.

Freedomways ran until the mid-1980s. Read straight through, its issues tell the story of the Black freedom movement as it unfolded, in the words of the people who made it.
