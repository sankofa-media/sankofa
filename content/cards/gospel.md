The blues-based style of Black gospel flourished in Chicago in the 1930s: new songs with sacred words, set to the melodies, harmonies and beat of the [[blues|blues]]. Unlike the [[spirituals|spirituals]], which no one person wrote, gospel songs had composers and were sold as sheet music. Choirs and soloists sang them, first to the piano and later to the Hammond organ, often to a strong, rocking beat. Many ministers at first turned the songs away: the beat sounded secular, like the blues clubs.

{male} The man called the father of gospel music first made his name in the [[blues|blues]]. Thomas A. Dorsey came to Chicago from Georgia in 1916 and, as "Georgia Tom", led Ma Rainey's band. Then he began setting sacred words to blues patterns and publishing the songs himself. In 1932 his wife Nettie died in childbirth, and their son died the next day. In his grief he wrote "[[precious-lord|Take My Hand, Precious Lord]]". In 1933 he co-founded a national convention of gospel choirs, and later teamed with Mahalia Jackson, who had come up from New Orleans. In 1968 she sang "Precious Lord" at the funeral of [[martin-luther-king|Martin Luther King Jr]]. Dorsey died in Chicago in 1993, having written hundreds of songs.

{female} The woman called the mother of gospel music could not read a note. Sallie Martin had cleaned houses and washed clothes in Atlanta before she came to Chicago in the 1920s and auditioned for Thomas A. Dorsey. He disliked her rough, shouting style but took her on. She sang his new songs in churches, sold the sheet music and kept his books, and within months his business turned a profit. She helped found the National Convention of Gospel Choirs and Choruses and travelled the country setting up choirs. In 1940 she and the composer Kenneth Morris opened Martin and Morris Music, which grew into one of the largest Black gospel publishers. Her Sallie Martin Singers were among the first all-female gospel groups. She died in Chicago in 1988.

From the churches the sound passed to [[rosetta-tharpe|Rosetta Tharpe]], who carried gospel and her guitar into the Cotton Club, and on into [[funk|funk]].
