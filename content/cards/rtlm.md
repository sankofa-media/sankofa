Radio Télévision Libre des Mille Collines, "Free Radio and Television of the Thousand Hills", was a private station that went on air in Kigali in the summer of 1993. Rwanda was at war with the Tutsi-led Rwandan Patriotic Front, and the station was set up by Hutu hard-liners who opposed President Juvénal Habyarimana's peace talks with the rebels.

It won listeners quickly. RTLM played the latest popular music between talk shows, and its presenters were lively and informal. Between the songs ran a steady stream of contempt for the Tutsi minority, whom the station called by the name of an insect.

When Habyarimana's plane was shot down in April 1994, the genocide began, and RTLM became one of its instruments. It read out lists of people to be killed and told the killers where victims were hiding. In about a hundred days some 800,000 people, most of them Tutsi, were murdered.

On 3 December 2003 the International Criminal Tribunal for Rwanda, in Arusha, convicted the station's leaders Ferdinand Nahimana and Jean-Bosco Barayagwiza, with Hassan Ngeze of the extremist paper Kangura, of genocide and incitement. The judges noted that no international court had dealt with the role of the media in this way since Nuremberg. On appeal in 2007 many convictions were overturned and sentences cut, but all three remained convicted.
