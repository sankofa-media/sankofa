Saint-Domingue, France's colony on Hispaniola, was in 1789 the richest colony in the Americas, its sugar and coffee grown by hundreds of thousands of enslaved Africans. In August 1791, after a ceremony at Bois Caïman, the enslaved of the northern plain rose. Under Toussaint Louverture they fought off British and Spanish armies. In 1802 Napoleon's army tried to restore slavery; Toussaint was seized and shipped to France, where he died in a mountain fortress in April 1803. Jean-Jacques Dessalines fought on, the French were beaten late in 1803, and on 1 January 1804 Dessalines proclaimed Haiti, a state founded by people who had freed themselves.

Then France sent a bill. In 1825 it demanded an indemnity of 150 million francs to compensate the former planters, later cut to 90 million. Haiti borrowed in France to pay; it made its last indemnity payment in 1878 and cleared that loan only in 1888. In 2022 the New York Times traced the payments over decades. A contested thesis by Susan Buck-Morss holds that Hegel built his master and slave out of the news from Haiti.

Maroons had held out in the hills of [[palmares|Brazil]] and [[nanny-of-the-maroons|Jamaica]]; here the enslaved took a whole colony and kept it. Its son [[general-dumas|Thomas-Alexandre Dumas]], born to an enslaved mother, commanded French armies.
