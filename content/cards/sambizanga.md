In 1961 Angola was a Portuguese colony on the brink of a war of independence, and Sambizanga, a district of Luanda, was home to many of the activists organising against colonial rule. Sambizanga, the 1972 film named after it, follows Domingos Xavier and his wife Maria. The colonial police take Domingos away. In prison he is beaten repeatedly and refuses to name his comrades. Maria, her baby on her back, walks from one prison to the next looking for him, until she learns that he has been beaten to death.

The director, Sarah Maldoror, was born in France to a Guadeloupean father and had co-founded Les Griots, the first theatre company in France made up of Black actors. She adapted a book by José Luandino Vieira with her partner Mário Pinto de Andrade, a leader of the MPLA, Angola's liberation movement. Angola was closed to her, so she shot in neighbouring Congo-Brazzaville, largely with non-actors, among them exiled Angolan activists.

The film won the Tanit d'Or at the Carthage Film Festival in 1972, while the Portuguese authorities kept it off screens in Angola. Now restored, it is seen again as one of the first feature films directed by a woman in Africa: a war film whose hero is a mother on foot.
