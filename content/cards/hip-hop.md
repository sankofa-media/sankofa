Hip-hop is a whole culture of DJing, rapping (MCing), breaking and graffiti, born in the Bronx in the early 1970s among young African American, Caribbean and Latino New Yorkers. The South Bronx was burning then: landlords paid arsonists to torch buildings for the insurance, and the near-bankrupt city cut fire and other basic services. For neglected young people, parties became an outlet and a voice. The DJ cut between records to stretch the instrumental break; the MC rhymed over the beat in rhythm and street speech.

{male} Clive Campbell grew up among the sound systems of Kingston, Jamaica; in the Bronx, as DJ Kool Herc, he played parties on his father's sound system. Seeing dancers wait for the drum breaks in funk and soul records, he switched between two copies of one record on two turntables to stretch the break: the merry-go-round. On the microphone he talked over the records, as Jamaican DJs did. The party usually dated as the start was his sister [[cindy-campbell|Cindy Campbell]]'s, in August 1973. Grandmaster Flash and Afrika Bambaataa built on his breaks and overshadowed him; he survived a stabbing and later drug addiction. In 2023 the Rock & Roll Hall of Fame inducted him for musical influence.

{female} In August 1973 [[cindy-campbell|Cindy Campbell]] needed money for school clothes. She threw a party in her building's recreation room at 1520 Sedgwick Avenue, wrote the invitations on index cards, charged girls 25 cents and boys 50, and asked her brother, DJ Kool Herc, to play. It raised a few hundred dollars; the parties soon outgrew the room. In 1979 Sylvia Robinson, who had sung the 1950s hit "Love Is Strange" and whose label faced bankruptcy, watched a Harlem club go wild as its DJ talked over the music. She put together the Sugarhill Gang and produced "Rapper's Delight", rap's first hit, on her new Sugar Hill label. In 2022 she entered the Rock & Roll Hall of Fame.

Later, drum machines and samples, including funk, became its instruments. Its roots are [[reggae|reggae]] and [[funk|funk]]; among the breaks it looped into fame is [[amen-break|the Amen break from "Amen, Brother"]].
