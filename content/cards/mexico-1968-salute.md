At the Mexico City Olympics in October 1968, Tommie Smith and John Carlos, Black sprinters from San José State in California, won gold and bronze in the 200 metres. Smith ran 19.83 seconds, a world record and the first 200 metres under 20 seconds. Between them, in silver, was the Australian Peter Norman.

On the podium Smith and Carlos stood in black socks without shoes, for Black poverty, bowed their heads during the anthem and each raised one black-gloved fist. They had only one pair of gloves; it was Norman who suggested they take one each. Norman, who was white, joined them by wearing the badge of the Olympic Project for Human Rights on his chest, as they did.

The US Olympic Committee ordered Smith and Carlos to leave Mexico, and at home they met a storm of fury. Norman's 20.06 seconds still stands as Australia's national record. The three stayed friends for life: when Norman died in Melbourne in 2006, Smith and Carlos carried his coffin. The photograph of the three men became one of the best-known images of the civil rights era.
