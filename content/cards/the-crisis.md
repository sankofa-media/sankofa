The Crisis is the magazine of the National Association for the Advancement of Colored People (NAACP), the American civil rights organisation. Its first issue came out in November 1910, edited by [[du-bois-infographics|W. E. B. Du Bois]], who wrote that it would show "the danger of race prejudice".

It found readers fast. The Crisis denounced lynching issue after issue, and by 1919 its monthly circulation had passed 100,000 copies.

From 1919 to 1926 its literary editor was Jessie Redmon Fauset. She sought out and promoted a generation of young writers, among them Langston Hughes, Countee Cullen and Jean Toomer, and printed their work. In 1921 The Crisis printed Hughes's poem "The Negro Speaks of Rivers". In 1919 it announced a magazine for children, [[brownies-book|The Brownies' Book]], which Du Bois and Fauset launched in 1920.

Du Bois edited The Crisis for its first 24 years and resigned in 1934. By then, for the young writers of the Harlem Renaissance, it had been one of the main doors into print.
