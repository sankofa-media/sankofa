Jerk is Jamaica's way of seasoning, smoking and grilling meat. Its story begins in 1655, when the English attacked Spanish Jamaica. The Spanish fled, and the Africans they had enslaved escaped into the mountains, becoming the first Maroons. In the hills they met the surviving Taíno, and the two peoples shared their ways with food. Maroon cooks hunted wild hog, rubbed it with pimento, salt and hot peppers, wrapped it in leaves and cooked it slowly in a covered pit in the ground. The pit was a matter of survival: the smoke of an open fire could lead British soldiers straight to a hidden settlement. Spice and slow smoke also preserved the meat, food for people who might have to move on at any moment. Modern jerk adds thyme, garlic, scallions and more, but pimento and Scotch bonnet peppers remain its heart. The cooking of [[nanny-of-the-maroons|the Maroons]] has travelled wherever Jamaicans have settled and is now on menus from the United States to Canada and Britain.
