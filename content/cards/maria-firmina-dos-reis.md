Maria Firmina dos Reis was born in São Luís, in the Brazilian province of Maranhão, in 1822 or 1825 — the records disagree — in an empire where slavery would last until 1888. She grew up with relatives in the small town of Guimarães, and in 1847 she won a public examination to teach primary school there.

In 1859 she published a novel, Úrsula, signed only "Uma maranhense", a woman of Maranhão. Its preface says the book is worth little, "because written by a woman, and a Brazilian woman" — and publishes it anyway. Inside, the enslaved speak for themselves: old Susana remembers her life in Africa and the crossing in the hold of a slave ship, which she calls a tomb. Úrsula is now counted as the first novel by a Black woman in Brazil.

She went on writing: the story "A Escrava" in 1887, a hymn for the liberation of the enslaved in 1888. After retiring she opened a free school at Maçaricó where boys and girls studied together, the first of its kind in Maranhão; it did not last. She died in Guimarães in 1917. Her novel was forgotten until researchers brought it back, and a facsimile came out in 1975.
