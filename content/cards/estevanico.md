In the 1520s Spain was trying to claim the lands around the Gulf of Mexico. Estevanico, also called Esteban, came from Azemmour on Morocco's Atlantic coast and was enslaved by a Spanish gentleman, Andrés Dorantes. In 1528 he landed in Florida with Dorantes as part of the expedition of Pánfilo de Narváez.

The expedition fell apart. The survivors built makeshift boats, and the one carrying Estevanico ran aground on the Texas coast near Galveston. Local peoples enslaved the castaways; four of them escaped together, Estevanico, Dorantes, Alonso Castillo and Álvar Núñez Cabeza de Vaca, and walked across Texas and northern Mexico. Along the way Native communities fed and honoured them as healers. In 1536, eight years after the landing, they reached Spanish Mexico and its capital.

In 1539 the viceroy, Antonio de Mendoza, sent Estevanico north as guide and scout for the friar Marcos de Niza, who was looking for the rumoured Seven Cities of Cíbola. Ranging far ahead of the friar, Estevanico reached the Zuni town of Hawikuh, in today's New Mexico, and was killed there. A year later Coronado's army followed the friar's reports north.

Estevanico was the first African known to cross Texas and one of the first people from the Old World to explore the American Southwest.
