"Respect" is a soul song that began as a man's demand and became one of the anthems of 1960s America. Otis Redding wrote it and released it in 1965 on the Stax label; in his version it is the man who asks his woman for respect.

Aretha Franklin, a Baptist minister's daughter who had grown up singing in her father's Detroit church, knew his record. In 1967, at an Atlantic session in New York with the producer Jerry Wexler, she recut it. With her sister Carolyn she reworked the tempo and phrasing, turned the song into a woman's demand, and added the call "sock it to me".

Her version went to number one on the Billboard pop chart. In June 1967 Redding sang the song at the Monterey Pop Festival and introduced it as a song a girl had taken away from him, adding that he would sing it anyway. In December of that year he died in a plane crash.

Franklin said that everyone wanted respect. Her record was heard as a woman's declaration and as a battle cry of the civil rights movement, and it remained her signature song.
