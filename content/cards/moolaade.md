Moolaadé is a 2004 film written and directed by the Senegalese Ousmane Sembène, maker of [[black-girl-sembene|Black Girl]], about the cutting of girls in a West African village, a ritual the villagers call purification. It was shot in a village in Burkina Faso, and its title means protection.

Collé Ardo Gallo Sy, a mother, had years earlier kept her daughter from the ritual. Now four girls run from the ceremony and ask her for moolaadé, a magical protection, and Collé grants it by stretching a coloured cord across the entrance to her compound. No one dares to step over it. The village is thrown into turmoil, and the elders are outraged. In retaliation, the men take away the women's radios, and Collé's husband whips her in public. She does not give in.

At Cannes in 2004 Moolaadé won the Un Certain Regard prize. Sembène died three years later, in 2007. His film had told the fight over the cutting of girls through a single village and a single woman's refusal.
