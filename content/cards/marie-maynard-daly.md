Marie Maynard Daly was born in Queens, New York, in 1921. Her father had studied chemistry at Cornell but could not afford to finish; her mother read aloud to her, and books such as Paul de Kruif's Microbe Hunters drew her to science. She took her chemistry degree at Queens College in 1942, a master's at New York University the next year, and at Columbia joined the laboratory of Mary Caldwell, studying how the digestive enzyme amylase turns cornstarch into sugar. Her PhD in 1947, earned in under three years, made her the first Black woman in the United States with a doctorate in chemistry.

After teaching at Howard University she won a grant from the American Cancer Society that took her to Alfred Mirsky's laboratory at the Rockefeller Institute, where for seven years she studied the chemistry of the cell nucleus. Back at Columbia from 1955, she ran studies linking high cholesterol to clogged arteries and heart disease. In 1960 she moved to the Albert Einstein College of Medicine, where she taught until 1986 and worked to bring more minority students into science. In 1988 she founded a scholarship at Queens College in her parents' names. She died in 2003.
