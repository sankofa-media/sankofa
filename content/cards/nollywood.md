In early-1990s Nigeria, in years of hard economic times, a film made straight for video started an industry. Kenneth Nnebue put an original story, written with Okechukwu Ogunjiofor, onto VHS: Living in Bondage (1992). Shot in Igbo for 150,000 naira, it follows Andy Okeke, who joins an occult society and sacrifices his wife for wealth, then is haunted by her ghost; tales of ritual money were in the air.

It sold at least 100,000 copies on tape. Nnebue had already made Yoruba-language films; the Igbo one is the one remembered as Nollywood's first blockbuster. The industry it started grew so fast that in 2009 a UNESCO survey ranked Nigeria second in the world, ahead of Hollywood, by the number of films it turned out, all on video. In 2019 the story returned, in cinemas this time, as a sequel: Living in Bondage: Breaking Free.
