In the 1960s Marie Van Brittan Brown was a nurse living in Jamaica, a neighbourhood of Queens in New York. Her shifts were long and often ended late at night; her husband Albert, an electronics technician, also kept irregular hours, so she was frequently home alone in an area with a high crime rate. She wanted to know who was at the door before she opened it.

The couple designed an answer together and filed for a patent on 1 August 1966. A television camera was mounted on the inside of the front door and slid up and down on a rail, so that it could look out at visitors of different heights. The picture went to a monitor inside the home. A microphone and loudspeaker let her talk to whoever stood outside, a radio-controlled lock opened the door from a distance, and an alarm could summon a guard or the police. United States patent 3,482,037, "Home security system utilizing television surveillance", was granted in both their names on 2 December 1969.

Brown died in 1999, aged 76. The camera at the door, the screen inside and the remote release of the lock in her design paved the way for the video security systems in use today, from apartment buildings to offices and banks.
