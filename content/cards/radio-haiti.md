Radio Haïti-Inter, in the country born of the [[haitian-revolution|Haitian Revolution]], was the first station to give the news in Creole, the language most Haitians speak. The agronomist Jean Dominique bought it and ran it in Port-au-Prince with Michèle Montas, his wife and co-anchor.

{male} Dominique had trained as an agronomist, and he took the side of the peasantry and the poor. His station carried microphones into the countryside and championed the stories of the landless.

The Duvalier dictatorship raided the station on 28 November 1980 and the couple went into exile.

{female} Montas was jailed along with the station's reporters, who were tortured, and was then expelled to the United States.

When Jean-Claude Duvalier fled in 1986, thousands met them at the airport, and they rebuilt the station with the help of ordinary Haitians. After the 1991 coup they fled again, returning in 1994.

On 3 April 2000 Dominique was shot dead outside the station with its guard, Jean-Claude Louissaint; who ordered the killing has never been established in court.

{male} Montas kept his station on air until threats closed it in 2003. The tapes on which he spoke to Haiti in its own language are open online at Duke.

{female} After the murder Montas ran the station, opening with "Bonjour, Jean", as she had while he was alive; every day she greeted him and counted the days since. On Christmas Day 2002 gunmen at her house killed her bodyguard, Maxime Séide. On 22 February 2003 she took the station off the air: "We have already lost three lives, we refuse to lose more." In exile she became, in 2007, spokesperson for UN Secretary-General Ban Ki-moon. The station's tapes are open online at Duke.
