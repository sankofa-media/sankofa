In March 1900 the British governor Frederick Hodgson held a durbar in Kumasi, capital of the Asante kingdom in what is now Ghana, whose king Prempeh the British had exiled. There he asked the chiefs to hand over the Golden Stool, which holds the soul of the Asante nation; later accounts say he demanded to sit on it, a version his wife's memoir disputes. Yaa Asantewaa, Queen Mother of Ejisu, led the rising that followed, and Asante fighters besieged the British in the fort at Kumasi. Within months a British expeditionary force put the revolt down. She was captured with other leaders and exiled to the Seychelles, where she died in 1921. Prempeh was allowed home only in 1924. In Ghana the war of 1900 is named after her, the Yaa Asantewaa War.
