Katwe is the largest of the slums of Kampala, Uganda's capital. There a coach named Robert Katende ran a chess programme for children, and the children who came were given porridge. Phiona Mutesi's family often had no food at home. Her brother went to the programme for a cup of porridge, and she followed him. She had never seen or heard of chess; there is no word for it in Luganda, her language.

She turned out to be a natural and an aggressive player. In 2010, as a teenager, she played for Uganda's women's team at the Chess Olympiad in Khanty-Mansiysk, in Siberia. Two years later, at the Olympiad in Istanbul, she scored 4½ points from nine games, the 50 per cent the rules required, and earned the title of Woman Candidate Master from the World Chess Federation. She and her teammate Ivy Amoko became the first women in Ugandan chess to hold a title.

A game she first came to for a cup of porridge took a girl from one of Kampala's poorest neighbourhoods to the world's chess boards, and into the record books of her country.
