Circus (Tsirk) is a Soviet musical comedy of 1936, directed by Grigori Aleksandrov in the Stalin years, and it became one of the best-loved Soviet films. Lyubov Orlova plays Marion Dixon, an American circus star who has fled racist persecution at home because her son is Black, and who finds a new life in a Moscow circus.

The boy, Jimmy, was played by three-year-old James Patterson. His father, Lloyd Patterson, was an African American who came to the USSR in 1932 with a group of Black Americans, drawn, like [[oliver-golden|Oliver Golden]], by its promise of racial equality; his mother, Vera Aralova, was a Soviet theatre artist. In the finale the circus audience sings the child a lullaby, and the actor Solomon Mikhoels, holding him, sings a verse in Yiddish.

James grew up to graduate from the Nakhimov naval school in Riga, served in the Black Sea Fleet and became a poet in the Soviet Writers' Union. After the Soviet Union collapsed he and his mother moved to Washington, D.C.

Made as propaganda against American racism, the film put a Black child and a Yiddish song at the emotional centre of a Soviet favourite.
