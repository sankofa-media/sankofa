In the late sixteenth century Japan was ending a century of civil war, and the warlord Oda Nobunaga was close to uniting the country. In 1579 the Italian Jesuit Alessandro Valignano landed on an inspection tour, and with him came a tall African man, his bodyguard or servant. His birth name is lost; the Japanese knew him as Yasuke.

When the mission reached Kyoto, crowds packed the streets to see him. Nobunaga summoned him and, not believing the colour was real, had him stripped to the waist and scrubbed. The scrubbing changed nothing, and Nobunaga, fascinated, took him into his own service. Yasuke, over six feet tall, received a house and a sword and served as his bodyguard.

The story lasted barely a year. In June 1582 Nobunaga's general Akechi Mitsuhide attacked him at the Honnō-ji temple in Kyoto, and Nobunaga took his own life. Yasuke made his way to Nobunaga's heir Nobutada at nearby Nijō, where the last defenders fought on until Nobutada too took his own life. Yasuke was taken; Akechi spared him and handed him back to the Jesuits. After that he vanishes from the record.

His brief rise in Nobunaga's household is recorded in both Japanese chronicles and Jesuit letters: a rare documented life of an African in sixteenth-century Japan.
