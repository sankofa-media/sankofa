In the eighteenth century the diamond contractor João Fernandes de Oliveira held the monopoly on diamonds around Tejuco, today's Diamantina, in Minas Gerais. [[chica-da-silva|Chica da Silva]], born enslaved, was freed by him and lived with him as his partner, and her story grew into a Brazilian legend.

In 1976 the director Carlos "Cacá" Diegues turned the legend into a period film, written with the writer João Felício dos Santos. Zezé Motta played Xica: an enslaved woman whom the contractor João Fernandes falls for and raises to be queen of the diamond district, until the king of Portugal sends an emissary to end her power. [[mas-que-nada|Jorge Ben]] wrote the title song. The film was a huge box-office hit, the biggest of Diegues's career, and her Xica made Motta famous.

It also started a fight. In October 1976 the paper Opinião gave four pages to the film. The historian Beatriz Nascimento wrote that it repeated the old stereotypes of Black people and belonged on an index of forbidden works; other critics said it reduced a Black woman to a sexual object.

Historians have since shown how far the film's seductress was from the real woman. In 2026 a 4K restoration returned the film to Brazilian cinemas.
