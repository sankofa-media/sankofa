In the 1820s southern Africa's high veld was torn by the wars and forced migrations that the Sotho call the Lifaqane, the Mfecane in Zulu. Moshoeshoe, born Lepoqo about 1786 at Menkhoaneng in what is now northern Lesotho, then led only a small band of followers. He took the name Moshoeshoe after a successful raid: it imitates the sound of a razor.

His first stronghold, Butha-Buthe, came under attack, among others from Sekonyela and the Batlokwa of [[mmanthatisi|Mmanthatisi]]. In 1824 he led his people to Thaba Bosiu, a flat-topped hill ringed by cliffs. Its name means "mountain at night", and tradition says that after dark it grew into a far more formidable fortress. When Mzilikazi's Ndebele failed to storm it, Moshoeshoe sent the retreating army a gift of cattle.

Refugees streamed to the mountain, and from them he formed a new people, the Basotho. He fought and bargained with Boers and British; in 1868, after defeats by the Orange Free State, he asked for British protection and his land became the British territory of Basutoland. He died on 11 March 1870. His kingdom survived inside British rule: out of the refugees of a catastrophe he had made a nation.
