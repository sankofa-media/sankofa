In the early 1970s no radio station in New York belonged to Black owners. Percy Sutton, born in San Antonio, Texas, in 1920, had served with the Tuskegee Airmen in the Second World War, then built a law practice whose clients included [[malcolm-x|Malcolm X]]. In 1966 he became Manhattan borough president.

While still in that office he and his partners bought the AM station WLIB, making it the first Black-owned radio station in New York. Their company, Inner City Broadcasting, added WBLS-FM and came to own some eighteen radio stations around the country.

In 1977 Sutton left office to run for mayor, lost, and turned to business. In 1981 he and his partners bought the crumbling Apollo Theater on 125th Street, Harlem's most famous stage. It reopened in 1985 after a renovation that gave it a television studio, the home of the variety show It's Showtime at the Apollo. Sutton died in December 2009, at 89. His station had shown that Harlem could own the voice that spoke to it.
