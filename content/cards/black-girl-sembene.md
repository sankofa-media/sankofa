Black Girl is a feature film made in 1966 in Senegal, a country only recently independent of France. Its director, Ousmane Sembène, had been a soldier in the Second World War and a docker and union militant in Marseille, taught himself to write and published novels, and then studied film in Moscow. He based his first feature on one of his own short stories.

Diouana, a young woman from Dakar, is hired by a French couple to look after their children. When they take her to Antibes on the Riviera, she is made to cook and clean as well, and the flat closes in on her. She brings her employers an African mask as a gift; later she takes it back. The French title, La Noire de..., means "the Black girl of...", leaving open whether she is from somewhere or belongs to someone. The story ends with her suicide. Diouana was played by Mbissine Thérèse Diop, a newcomer to acting.

The film is usually counted the first feature by a director from sub-Saharan Africa. Sembène, often called the father of African cinema, went on making films about Senegal until his death in 2007.
