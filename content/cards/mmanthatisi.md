In the early 19th century the Batlokwa were a Sotho people living in what is now the Free State province of South Africa. Mmanthatisi, a daughter of a chief of the Basia, married their ruler Mokotjo. When he died their son Sekonyela was still a boy, and she became regent with full authority over the Batlokwa.

Then the Mfecane wars reached the high veld. The Hlubi, themselves driven from their land, attacked the Batlokwa and pushed them from their homeland. Mmanthatisi led her people on a long migration of raids and battles. She is credited with planning the campaigns, and her people became known as the "Mantatees" after her.

In 1824, with Sekonyela now at her side, she besieged [[moshoeshoe|Moshoeshoe]] at Butha-Buthe, and he left for the mountain of Thaba Bosiu. That same year she handed power to her son. The Batlokwa settled in the Caledon valley, in what is now Lesotho, and she lived out her days in a hilltop stronghold there.

Through the worst years of war on the high veld, a woman ruled the Batlokwa.
