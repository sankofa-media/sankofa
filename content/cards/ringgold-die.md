American People Series #20: Die is an oil painting that Faith Ringgold made in New York in 1967. Born in Harlem in 1930, she was then working through her American People series, canvases on race in America, while riots broke out in city after city.

The older painter Hale Woodruff had criticised her work for lacking movement, and as she remembered it, she set out to show him she knew rhythm and movement. Die was her answer. On a monumental canvas, Black and white men and women in suits and cocktail dresses, bloodied, fight or flee in a single melee, and at the centre two children, a white boy and a Black girl, huddle together. Ringgold drew on Picasso's Guernica and on the riot scenes of [[migration-series|Jacob Lawrence's Migration Series]]. She first showed it in 1967, at a gallery opening on 57th Street in Manhattan.

Ringgold went on to become famous for her story quilts, such as Tar Beach. The Museum of Modern Art acquired Die in 2016.

The painting refused to look away from 1967, and it made a Black woman's history painting part of the canon it had been left out of.
