American People Series #20: Die is an oil painting that Faith Ringgold made in New York in 1967. Born in Harlem in 1930, she was then painting her American People series, canvases about race in America, while race riots broke out in American cities.

That year Robert Newman, who ran the Spectrum Gallery on 57th Street in Manhattan, invited her to show the series there, her first solo exhibition, and let her use the gallery as a studio. Die was the result: a canvas twelve feet long, painted on two panels, on which Black and white men and women in the formal middle-class clothes of the day fight or flee in one bloody melee, while two frightened children cling to each other. Ringgold drew on Picasso's Guernica.

Ringgold went on to become famous for her story quilts, such as Tar Beach, and in 2016 the Museum of Modern Art acquired Die.

The painting refused to look away from 1967, and nearly half a century later a Black woman's history painting of American violence took its place in the Museum of Modern Art.
