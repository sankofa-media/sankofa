Butetown, the dock district of Cardiff better known as Tiger Bay, was where the sea brought the world to Wales. From the nineteenth century seafarers from Somalia, Yemen, other parts of Africa and the Caribbean settled there, and the neighbourhood became one of the first multicultural communities in Britain.

The First World War ended in a scramble for work as demobilised soldiers came home. In June 1919 race riots broke out in British port cities, Cardiff among them, and the anger over jobs turned on the Black and minority residents of the docks.

The community stayed. In 1934 Rachel Elizabeth "Betty" Johnson was born in Butetown. When she told the headmistress of her grammar school that she wanted to become a teacher, she was told it could not be done. In 1973, as Betty Campbell, she became head of Mount Stuart Primary in Butetown and the first Black headteacher in Wales. She taught her pupils about slavery, Black history and apartheid, and later helped launch Black History Month in Wales.

In September 2021 her statue was unveiled in Cardiff's Central Square: the first outdoor statue of a named Black person in Wales, a short walk from the streets of Tiger Bay.
