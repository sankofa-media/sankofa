Légitime Défense, "Self-Defence", was a journal that a group of students from Martinique published in Paris in 1932. Among them were Étienne Léro, René Ménil and Jules-Marcel Monnerot. Several of them had already written for [[nardal-sisters|La Revue du monde noir]] of Paulette Nardal.

Their journal took up the tools of the Paris avant-garde, surrealism and Marxism, and turned them on Martinique: on its society, its colonial order and its literature. Only one issue ever appeared. The French authorities quickly banned it.

Its readers did not forget it. Aimé and Suzanne Césaire, together with René Ménil, returned to Martinique, and in 1941, with the island under the Vichy regime, they founded the journal Tropiques. Scholars count Légitime Défense as the beginning of Black Caribbean surrealism and of the first debates about Martinican identity. Three years after it, Césaire and his friends started the student paper [[etudiant-noir|L'Étudiant noir]]. One thin issue had started a conversation that ran through the journals of the Négritude generation.
