Treemonisha is an opera in three acts by Scott Joplin, the composer of "Maple Leaf Rag" and "The Entertainer", who had grown up in north-east Texas and settled in New York. He wrote words and music. Publishers wanted his rags, not an opera, so in 1911 he printed the piano-vocal score at his own expense.

The story is set on a plantation in Arkansas, near Texarkana. A couple find a baby girl under a tree and name her Treemonisha; she grows up to be the one educated person in her community. Conjurers who profit from its superstitions kidnap her, she is rescued, and the community makes her its leader. Its theme is education, for women as much as men.

Joplin could not raise the money for a staging. In 1915 he played it at the piano for possible backers; nothing followed. He died in a Manhattan state hospital in April 1917.

A revival of interest in Joplin in the 1970s brought it back. The Atlanta Symphony gave the first full staging in 1972, directed by Katherine Dunham, and Houston Grand Opera gave it a full professional production in 1975. In 1976 Joplin was awarded a posthumous Pulitzer Prize for it. An opera that put a young Black woman's learning at its centre had waited sixty years for a stage.
