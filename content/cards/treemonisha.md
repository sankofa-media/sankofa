Treemonisha is an opera in three acts by Scott Joplin, the composer of "Maple Leaf Rag" and "The Entertainer", who was born in north-east Texas and lived in New York. He wrote the words as well as the music. No company would take the risk of staging it, and in 1911 he paid to publish the piano-vocal score himself.

The story is set on a plantation in Arkansas, near Texarkana. A couple take in a foundling girl and name her Treemonisha; she grows up to be the one educated person in her community. Conjurers who live off its superstitions kidnap her, she is rescued, and the community makes her its leader. Joplin believed education was the way forward for Black Americans, and here a young woman carries that belief.

In 1915 he played it at the piano for possible backers, and nothing came of it. He died in a Manhattan state hospital in April 1917.

A revival of interest in Joplin in the 1970s brought the opera back. The Atlanta Symphony gave the first full staging in 1972, and Houston Grand Opera a professional production in 1975. In 1976 Joplin was awarded a posthumous Pulitzer Prize for it. An opera that put a young Black woman's learning at its centre had waited sixty years for a stage.
