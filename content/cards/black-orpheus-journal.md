Black Orpheus was a literary journal founded in 1957 in Ibadan, Nigeria, a few years before independence, when African writing in English was still a thin stream. Its founder, Ulli Beier, was a German who had come to Ibadan's university college to teach phonetics; another German, Janheinz Jahn, was with him at the start. The journal printed poetry, fiction, art and criticism, and its early issues carried translations of the French-language poets of Négritude, such as Léopold Sédar Senghor and Aimé Césaire.

In time Beier handed the journal to the young Nigerian writers around it, and its editors came to include Wole Soyinka and Christopher Okigbo. In Ibadan he also founded the Mbari club, a meeting place for artists and writers, and in Oshogbo he worked with the dramatist Duro Ladipo and the painters of a new local art movement.

Shortly before the civil war of 1967, Beier left Nigeria for Papua New Guinea. Black Orpheus had made African writers visible to one another across languages and borders, and in 2025 the platform OlongoAfrica scanned its surviving issues and put them online for anyone to read.
