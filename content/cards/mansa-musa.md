In the fourteenth century Mali was a West African empire rich in gold, and in 1324 its ruler, Mansa Musa, carried that gold across the Sahara. On his way to Mecca he stopped in Cairo and gave so much away that, as the writer al-Umari recorded, it flooded the market and depressed the value of gold in Egypt. He came home with scholars and an Andalusian architect, and Timbuktu gained its great Djinguereber mosque; the city's learning lives on in the [[timbuktu-manuscripts|Timbuktu manuscripts]]. Europe heard of him too. Half a century later a Majorcan mapmaker painted "Musse Melly" on the Catalan Atlas: crowned, holding a gold disc, the richest king of the region. The empire he ruled had been founded a century earlier by [[sunjata|Sunjata Keita, still sung in the epic that bears his name]].
