The Spiritual Baptists are a faith born in Trinidad in the 19th century. They join Protestant Christianity with West African ways of worship: bells, hand-clapping, foot-stamping, loud singing and shouting. Their critics called them Shouters. Among their roots are the Baptist congregations of the [[merikins|Merikins]], Black American veterans of the War of 1812 who settled in south Trinidad.

The British colonial government found the worship too noisy and too African. On 28 November 1917 its Shouters Prohibition Ordinance came into force. It made it an offence to hold, attend or take part in a Shouters' meeting of two or more people, indoors or out, by day or night, to keep a Shouters' house, or to initiate anyone into the faith. For thirty-four years believers faced the police, the courts, fines and prison with hard labour.

In the 1930s the labour leader Tubal Uriah "Buzz" Butler demanded repeal, and the Spiritual Baptist preacher Elton George Griffith carried the campaign on. On 30 March 1951 the ordinance was repealed. In 1996 the government of Basdeo Panday made 30 March a public holiday, Spiritual Shouter Baptist Liberation Day, and on it the bells now ring openly across the country.
