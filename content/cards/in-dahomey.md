In Dahomey is a musical comedy that opened on Broadway in 1903, with music by Will Marion Cook and lyrics by the poet Paul Laurence Dunbar. Its stars were the comedy team of Bert Williams and George Walker, and every member of its cast was Black.

Bert Williams worked inside the conventions of minstrelsy, in blackface, playing a bumbling clown, a stock role built on the racist stereotypes of the day. Around him stood the first Broadway musical written and performed by African Americans, and it was a success.

In May 1903 the company took it to the Shaftesbury Theatre in London. London audiences came for the cakewalk, and Williams and Walker danced it for them. A shortened version was given as a command performance for the king at Buckingham Palace. The show then toured Britain into 1904.

Back home, Williams and Walker went on to Bandanna Land in 1908. In Dahomey remains a landmark of Black theatre, the show that carried a Black company from Broadway to Buckingham Palace.
