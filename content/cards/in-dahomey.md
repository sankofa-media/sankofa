In Dahomey is a musical comedy that opened on Broadway, at the New York Theatre, in 1903, with music by Will Marion Cook and lyrics by the poet Paul Laurence Dunbar. Its stars were the comedy team of Bert Williams and George Walker, and every member of its cast was Black.

Bert Williams worked inside the conventions of minstrelsy and vaudeville, playing the slow-witted stock character that was then a standard, if racist, comic role. Around him stood a show written, composed and played by Black artists, and it was a Broadway success.

In May 1903 the company took it to the Shaftesbury Theatre in London. Londoners expected the cakewalk, and Williams and Walker's version caused a sensation in the city's dance halls. A shortened version was given as a command performance for the king at Buckingham Palace. It then toured Britain into 1904.

Back home, Williams and Walker went on to Bandanna Land in 1908. In Dahomey set a standard for Black song, dance and comedy on the London stage that lasted into the 1920s.
