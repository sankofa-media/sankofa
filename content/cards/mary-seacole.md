Mary Seacole was born in Kingston, Jamaica, in 1805, the daughter of a Scottish soldier and a free Black Jamaican woman who healed the sick at her boarding house. From her mother she learned Creole herbal medicine, and she called herself a "doctress". She nursed yellow fever patients in Jamaica and cholera victims in Panama.

In 1854 Britain went to war with Russia in Crimea, and London read reports of soldiers left without care. Seacole offered to serve as an army nurse and was refused; she put it down to prejudice against her skin. She resolved to go "upon my own responsibility and at my own cost". In 1855, with Thomas Day, a relative of her late husband, she set up the British Hotel near Balaclava, a store and canteen that sold food, supplies and medicines to the army. She also went out to the front to tend the wounded, who called her "Mother Seacole".

Peace left her with unsold stock and debts, and she was declared bankrupt. Her memoir, Wonderful Adventures of Mrs Seacole in Many Lands, came out in 1857 and became a bestseller. She died in London in 1881 and was soon forgotten; today she is widely remembered again as a healer who would not take no for an answer.
