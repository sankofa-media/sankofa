Within Our Gates is a silent drama of 1920 and the oldest known surviving feature film by an African American director. Oscar Micheaux, born in 1884 in Metropolis, Illinois, had been a Pullman porter and a South Dakota homesteader, then wrote a book about it, The Homesteader. When a Black film company sought the rights, he filmed it himself.

The film answered D. W. Griffith's The Birth of a Nation (1915), the racist epic of its day. Evelyn Preer plays Sylvia Landry, a young Southern woman who goes north to raise money for a school for poor Black children. In a flashback a white mob lynches a Black man. Chicago's censors at first refused to pass it, and only after a delay did it open in the city. Prints were soon cut over the lynching scenes, and white theatre owners in the South refused to book it.

Then the film vanished for decades, until a single print titled La Negra turned up in the Filmoteca Española in Madrid. It came to the Library of Congress in 1990 and was restored there. The original English intertitles were almost all lost, so they were translated back from Spanish.

Five years after Griffith, Micheaux had answered him on the screen, and the answer survived by a thread: one print in a Spanish archive.
