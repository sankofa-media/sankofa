Within Our Gates is a silent drama of 1920 and the oldest known surviving feature film by an African American director. Oscar Micheaux, born in 1884 in Metropolis, Illinois, had been a Pullman porter and a South Dakota homesteader, then wrote a book about it, The Homesteader. When a Black film company sought the rights, he filmed it himself.

His second feature answered D. W. Griffith's The Birth of a Nation (1915), the racist epic of its day. Evelyn Preer plays Sylvia Landry, a young Southern woman who goes north to raise money for a school for poor Black children. In a flashback a white mob lynches a Black man. Chicago's censor board took two months to pass the film, which opened there in January 1920. Prints were soon cut over the lynching scenes, and white theatre owners in the South refused to book it.

Then the film vanished for decades, until a film scholar identified a print titled La Negra in the Filmoteca Española in Madrid. It came to the Library of Congress in 1990 and was restored in 1993. Only four of the original English intertitles survived, so the rest were translated back from Spanish.

Five years after Griffith, Micheaux had shown a lynching from the side of its victims, and the proof survived by a thread.
