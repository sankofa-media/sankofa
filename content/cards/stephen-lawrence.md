On the night of 22 April 1993 Stephen Lawrence, 18, was waiting for a bus on Well Hall Road in Eltham, south-east London, with his friend Duwayne Brooks. A group of white youths crossed the road; one of them shouted a racist slur, and they surrounded Stephen and stabbed him twice. He ran over a hundred yards, collapsed and died.

No one was convicted. In 1996 his parents brought a private prosecution against three suspects; it collapsed, all three were acquitted, and the rule against double jeopardy barred any second trial. A public inquiry under Sir William Macpherson reported in February 1999. It condemned the police investigation and found "institutional racism", a collective failure to serve people properly because of their colour, culture or ethnic origin, in the Metropolitan Police and other institutions. It also asked whether that bar should be reconsidered.

Part 10 of the Criminal Justice Act 2003 allowed a retrial after acquittal for the gravest crimes when new and compelling evidence appeared. After advances in forensic science, in May 2011 the Court of Appeal quashed Gary Dobson's acquittal, and on 3 January 2012 Dobson and David Norris were convicted of Stephen's murder. A killing at a bus stop had made Britain name institutional racism and rewrite an ancient rule of its law.
