"Redemption Song" is a song from Uprising, the 1980 album that was the last Bob Marley released in his lifetime, and its best-known lines come from a speech given in Canada 43 years earlier.

The speaker was Marcus Garvey, the Jamaican who in 1914 founded the Universal Negro Improvement Association to preach Black pride and unity. Its branches spread far beyond Jamaica, and in 1918 one opened at Glace Bay, on Cape Breton Island in Nova Scotia. By 1937 Garvey's movement had dwindled and he had been deported from the United States. That year he set out on a speaking tour, and on 1 October he addressed the UNIA members of Sydney at Menelik Hall. There he said: "We are going to emancipate ourselves from mental slavery because whilst others might free the body, none but ourselves can free the mind."

Garvey died in London in 1940. Four decades later Marley, a follower of the [[pinnacle-rastafari|Rastafari]] faith, turned that passage into the heart of his song: "Emancipate yourselves from mental slavery, none but ourselves can free our minds." A sentence spoken to a UNIA branch in Cape Breton became one of the best-known lines in [[reggae]].
