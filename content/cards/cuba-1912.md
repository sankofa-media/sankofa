Cuba fought Spain for its independence from 1868 to 1898. In the new republic, Black and mixed-race veterans of those wars found jobs and public office closed to them. On 7 August 1908 two of those veterans, Evaristo Estenoz and Pedro Ivonnet, founded the Partido Independiente de Color, the Independent Party of Colour. It demanded that the republic deliver the racial equality it had promised, starting with fair access to public office.

In 1910 a law proposed by the senator Martín Morúa Delgado outlawed parties organised by race, and the party was banned. On 20 May 1912 Estenoz and Ivonnet led an armed protest, mainly in Oriente province, to win back its legal status. The government sent in the army. In Oriente the killing was worst: thousands of Afro-Cubans died, how many is still disputed. By July 1912 Ivonnet himself was dead, killed by government troops.

The killing went into the record as a "race war", a name historians have since challenged: what Afro-Cuban veterans had asked for was a share in the republic they had fought to create.
