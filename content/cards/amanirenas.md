In the 20s BC Kush, the Nubian kingdom on the Middle Nile that had once ruled Egypt under [[taharqa|Taharqa]], had its capital at Meroë. Its ruling queens bore the title kandake, which Greek and Roman writers turned into "Candace". When Augustus defeated Antony and Cleopatra and made Egypt a Roman province, Rome became Kush's neighbour.

In 25 BC, while part of the Roman garrison was away at war in Arabia, the Kushite army under the kandake Amanirenas took Syene (Aswan), Elephantine and Philae, enslaved their people and pulled down the statues of Augustus. Strabo, who wrote in her lifetime, calls her "a masculine sort of woman, and blind in one eye". The prefect Petronius struck back and razed Napata. The queen's forces attacked again, and the war ended in talks: her envoys reached Augustus on the island of Samos, obtained what they asked for, and the tribute Rome had imposed was cancelled.

Most of the statues went back, but one bronze head of Augustus did not. It was buried beneath the steps of a temple to Victory at Meroë, apparently so that its captors would tread on it for ever. Archaeologists later dug it up there, and it is now in the British Museum.
