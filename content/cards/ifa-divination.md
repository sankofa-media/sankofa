Ifá is the divination system of the Yoruba of south-western Nigeria, also practised by their descendants in the Americas and the Caribbean. It takes its name from Ifá, also called Orunmila, the Yoruba deity of wisdom. Ifá is consulted whenever an important decision has to be made, by a person or by a whole community. Its priest, the babalawo, does not fall into trance: he reads signs.

The babalawo casts sixteen sacred palm nuts, the ikin, or throws a divining chain. He records how they fall as marks in powder spread on a carved wooden tray, the opon Ifá, and he strikes the tray with a tapper to open a session. The marks come in only two kinds, so scholars describe the system as binary; together they form one of 256 possible signs, the odu.

Each odu opens onto its own verses, the ese, which the babalawo chants in poetic language to the person seeking guidance.

The system crossed the Atlantic: Ifá guides believers in Cuban [[lucumi|Santería]] and Brazilian [[candomble|Candomblé]]. In 2005 UNESCO proclaimed it a Masterpiece of the Oral and Intangible Heritage of Humanity.
