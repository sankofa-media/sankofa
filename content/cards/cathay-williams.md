After the Civil War the US Army raised new regiments of Black soldiers for the western frontier, the men later called Buffalo Soldiers. Women were not allowed to serve. Cathay Williams was born enslaved in Jackson County, Missouri, in 1844 by one account and around 1850 by another. In 1861 Union troops took her to cook and wash for officers, and she followed the army through the war, for a time with General Philip Sheridan's staff. On 15 November 1866 she enlisted as William Cathay and was assigned to the newly formed 38th Infantry, an all-Black regiment. She served at posts in New Mexico Territory, among them Fort Cummings and Fort Bayard, and kept her secret through repeated stays in hospital until the post surgeon found that she was a woman. On 14 October 1868 she was discharged on a surgeon's certificate of disability. She went back to her own name and lived in Pueblo and then Trinidad, Colorado, where in 1876 a St. Louis newspaper published her story. Around 1890, ill, she applied for an army pension as a disabled veteran; it was refused. When she died is not known for certain. She is the only African American woman documented as serving as a soldier in the Regular Army in the nineteenth century.
