Santería is a religion of African origin that took shape in Cuba in the nineteenth century, when hundreds of thousands of Yoruba people from West Africa were enslaved and shipped to the island. In Cuba their faith absorbed elements of Catholicism. Devotees expressed their love for the orishas, the Yoruba deities, through the images of Catholic saints, and the religion got its Spanish name, "the way of the saints". It is also known as the Yoruba or [[lucumi|Lukumí]] religion, after the name by which the Yoruba were known in Cuba.

Its heart is a personal bond between a devotee and the orishas, kept through offerings, sometimes animal sacrifice, and through initiation. In Cuba the faith was persecuted and practised in secret. After the revolution of 1959 Cuban exiles carried it to the United States.

Then the city of Hialeah, Florida, passed laws aimed at the animal sacrifices of a Santería congregation, the Church of the Lukumi Babalu Aye. The church went to court, and in 1993 the US Supreme Court unanimously struck the laws down, upholding the devotees' right to their rite.

A faith that once survived by hiding behind saints won its freedom in open court; the same Yoruba heritage lives on in Brazil's [[candomble|Candomblé]].
