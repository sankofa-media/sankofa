Victoria Santa Cruz is remembered in Peru as the mother of Afro-Peruvian dance. She was born in Lima in 1922, the eighth of ten children; her father was a playwright, and her mother danced the zamacueca and the marinera. In 1958 she and her younger brother Nicomedes founded Cumanana, Peru's first Black theatre company. There she began to shape her own method: recovering a lost culture through inner rhythm and what she called ancestral memory.

After studying theatre and choreography in Paris, she founded Teatro y Danzas Negras del Perú. The company danced in Lima theatres and on Peruvian television, and it represented Peru at the cultural festivities of the 1968 Mexico City Olympics. She re-created old Afro-Peruvian dances such as the landó and the zamacueca for the stage. From 1973 to 1982 she directed Peru's National Folklore Ensemble, and then she taught at Carnegie Mellon University in Pittsburgh until 1999.

In 1978 she performed Me gritaron negra, "They shouted 'Black girl!' at me", a spoken poem that turns a racial insult into pride. She died in Lima in 2014 and lay in state at the national museum. Her poem and her dances gave Black Peru a voice and a stage of its own.
