"Ol' Man River" is sung by Joe, a Black character, in Show Boat, the 1927 Broadway musical by Jerome Kern and Oscar Hammerstein II. An early script already pencils in Paul Robeson, the singer and actor, for the part of Joe, but he was not available for the Broadway premiere. He took the part in London in 1928 and in the 1932 Broadway revival.

Hammerstein's Joe is resigned: "I'm tired of livin' an' skeered of dyin'". In concert Robeson changed the words. "Get a little drunk an' you land in jail" became "You show a little grit and you lands in jail", and the weary lines became "I keeps laffin' instead of cryin', I must keep fightin' until I'm dyin'". A song about endurance turned into a song about resistance.

Robeson paid for his politics. In the McCarthy era he was blacklisted and his passport was revoked. He kept singing his version all the same, and in 1960 he sang it for the construction workers on the building site of the Sydney Opera House. A show tune written for him by others had become his own manifesto.
