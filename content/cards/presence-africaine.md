Présence Africaine is a review and a publishing house in Paris, founded by Alioune Diop. Diop was born in Saint-Louis, Senegal, in 1910. He studied classics in Algiers and then at the Sorbonne, and from 1943 he taught in French schools. After the war he spent a short time in politics, in Senegal and in France.

In 1947, with the war just over, he left politics and launched the review. He meant it as a platform where Africans could set out their own history and culture and take their place in the debates of the modern world. In 1949 Diop added a publishing house of the same name, for thinkers and writers from Africa and the diaspora.

In 1956 Présence Africaine organised the First Congress of Black Writers and Artists, held at the Sorbonne, the university where Diop had once studied. It brought artists and intellectuals from Africa and the diaspora together in the heart of the French capital.

Diop died in Paris on 2 May 1980. The review and the house outlived him.
