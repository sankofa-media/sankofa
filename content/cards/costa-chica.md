The Costa Chica, the "short coast", is the stretch of Mexico's Pacific shore that runs south-east from Acapulco in Guerrero into Oaxaca. It is home to most of Mexico's Afro-Mexicans. Their ancestors were Africans brought by the Spanish in slavery from the 16th century; many worked the colonial cattle ranches of the coast.

Modern Mexico long left them out of its picture of the nation and did not count them. Away from the coast Afro-Mexicans were often taken for foreigners in their own country.

They pushed for recognition, and it came in two steps. In 2015 a national survey for the first time allowed people to identify themselves as Black, Afro-Mexican or of African descent: 1.38 million did, 1.2 per cent of the population. On 9 August 2019 a new section C of Article 2 of the constitution was published. It recognises Afro-Mexican peoples and communities, "whatever they call themselves", as part of the pluricultural composition of the nation, with the rights the article already gave to Indigenous peoples.
