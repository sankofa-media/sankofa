Prince Nico Mbarga was born in Nigeria in 1950. When the Nigerian civil war broke out he was a teenager, and he spent the war years across the border in Cameroon, where his music took shape. Back in Nigeria in the 1970s he led a band called Rocafil Jazz, playing [[highlife]], the dance-band music of West Africa.

In 1974 the record company EMI turned down "Sweet Mother", a song in praise of a mother's love sung in Pidgin English, as too childish. Rocafil Jazz released it with another label in 1976, and it sold across the continent, with sales usually given as more than 13 million copies. Mbarga never had another hit on that scale.

He was killed in a motorcycle accident on 24 June 1997. Seven years later the BBC asked its African readers and listeners to choose Africa's favourite song from a shortlist of ten, and they chose "Sweet Mother". A song about the first love of every child had become what it is still called: Africa's anthem.
