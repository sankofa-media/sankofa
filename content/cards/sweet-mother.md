Prince Nico Mbarga was born in Nigeria in 1950. In the 1970s he led a band called Rocafil Jazz, which played [[highlife]], the dance-band music of West Africa.

In 1976 the band recorded "Sweet Mother". The song is a son's lifelong gratitude to the mother who struggled to raise him: when he was hungry, she ran up and down to find him something to eat. It was sung in Pidgin English, and it sold across the continent, with sales usually given as more than 13 million copies. Mbarga never had another hit on that scale.

He was killed in a motorcycle accident on 24 June 1997. Seven years later the BBC asked its African readers and listeners to choose Africa's favourite song from a shortlist of ten, and they chose "Sweet Mother". A song about the first love of every child had become what it is still called: Africa's anthem.
