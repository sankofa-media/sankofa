Coptic is the last stage of the language of ancient Egypt and its youngest written form. Earlier Egyptian had been written in hieroglyphs and in the cursive hieratic and Demotic scripts, but by the second century CE written Demotic no longer matched the spoken language and was falling out of use. Instead Egyptian came to be written in the Greek alphabet, the script in which Egypt's government and its Christian church worked. For sounds that Greek lacked they added six or seven letters, depending on the dialect, taken from Demotic, and Greek words became an important part of the vocabulary.

Coptic had several dialects, the best known being Sahidic and Bohairic. By about the fourteenth century writing in Coptic had given way to Arabic, but Bohairic Coptic lives on in the liturgy of the Coptic Church.

Centuries later it opened the past. Jean-François Champollion was fluent in Coptic. When he worked on the Rosetta Stone, which carries the same text in Greek, Demotic and hieroglyphs, his Coptic let him hear the Egyptian words behind the signs: he saw that hieroglyphs also stood for sounds. In 1822 he succeeded in reading them.

So the last spoken form of Egyptian became the bridge back to its first writing, and the pharaohs' language is still heard at Coptic services.
