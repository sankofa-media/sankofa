The Giriama live in the hinterland of Kenya's coast, where they farmed and traded. Early in the 20th century, under British rule, the colonial authorities wanted them as labour for the plantations Europeans were developing along the coast, and demanded hut tax.

Mekatilili wa Menza, a Giriama woman, travelled the country with the elder Wanje wa Mwadorikola, urging the Giriama to refuse to work for the British and to defend their own ways. Late in 1913 the British arrested both and sent them to Kisii, in the far west of Kenya, to serve five years.

In 1914 they escaped from Kisii and made their way back to the coast, but she was soon arrested again. That August the Giriama rose. British forces destroyed sacred kaya, the forest shrines of the Giriama, and military force broke the resistance. Mekatilili was held until 1919, when she and Wanje were freed.

A woman had led a people against the colonial order on the eve of the First World War. Today an annual festival on the Kenyan coast is held in her memory.
