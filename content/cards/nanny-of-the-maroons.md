In eighteenth-century British Jamaica, runaways from slavery built free communities in the mountains: the Maroons. Nanny led the Windward Maroons of the Blue Mountains, fighting the British with ambushes; Maroon memory adds legends, such as Nanny catching bullets, that no document supports.

In 1734 the British took Nanny Town, but her people regrouped and settled anew. When Captain Quao signed a peace treaty in 1739, she reportedly opposed it. The treaties secured Maroon freedom and land but obliged them to turn back new runaways.

What is on paper is a land patent of 1740, still in the Jamaica Archives, granting land to "Nanny" and her people. Historians believe she lived into the 1750s; the Moore Town Maroons honour her as their leader and keep her grave there, known as Bump Grave. Jamaica made her its first and only woman National Hero, and she is on the 500-dollar note.
