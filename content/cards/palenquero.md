Palenquero, which its speakers simply call "lengua", "the language", is spoken in San Basilio de Palenque, a village some 60 kilometres from Cartagena, Colombia. Most of its words come from Spanish, but its grammar does not: it bears the stamp of Central African Bantu languages, above all Kikongo, spoken by many of the village's founders. It is the only Spanish-based creole on the South American mainland.

In the 17th century people who escaped slavery around Cartagena built fortified refuges in the hinterland, called palenques. San Basilio grew out of one of them, as [[palmares|Palmares]] had grown from runaways in Brazil.

In the 20th century Palenqueros met ridicule and discrimination in schools and neighbouring towns, and young people spoke to one another more and more in Spanish. Linguists predicted the language would soon die. It did not: young Palenqueros took it up again as a sign of pride. In 2005 UNESCO proclaimed the cultural space of Palenque, with its music, healing and funeral rites and its language at the centre, a masterpiece of the oral and intangible heritage of humanity.

For some three hundred years the village has kept two languages side by side: Spanish for the outside world, and its own, made by the people who freed themselves.
