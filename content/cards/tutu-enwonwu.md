Tutu is a portrait that the Nigerian painter Ben Enwonwu (1917–1994), one of the leading modernists of his country, made in 1974. Its sitter was Adetutu Ademiluyi, a princess of the royal house of Ife, known as Tutu. Nigeria had just come through its civil war, the war over Biafran independence, and Enwonwu's three versions of the princess became a symbol of national reconciliation and a national icon, nicknamed the African Mona Lisa.

Then all three originals disappeared, and for decades their whereabouts were a mystery. Giles Peppiatt, director of modern African art at the auction house Bonhams, was regularly sent "Tutus" that turned out to be prints. Late in 2017 a family in north London asked him to come and look at a painting that had hung in their flat for thirty years. It was the real Tutu.

On 28 February 2018 Bonhams sold it in London for £1,205,000, more than four times the top estimate, with bidders in Lagos following the sale live. It was a record for Enwonwu. The other two versions were still missing.

A picture that had lived for forty years in reproductions came back as an original, and its price told the art market what modern African painting was worth.
