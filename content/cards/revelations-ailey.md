Alvin Ailey was born in 1931 in Rogers, a small town in Texas, to a single teenage mother. The two of them lived by picking cotton, washing clothes and domestic work in one Texas town after another, and the boy went to Baptist church. During the Second World War mother and son moved to Los Angeles, where Ailey found modern dance in the company of the choreographer Lester Horton.

In 1958, in New York, his own company, the Alvin Ailey American Dance Theater, gave its first concert. Two years later Ailey made Revelations, a dance set to traditional African American religious songs, [[spirituals|spirituals]] among them. It brought the drama of those songs onto the concert stage.

Revelations became Ailey's best-known work and the signature of his company. Ailey received the Kennedy Center Honors in 1988 and died in New York a year later. The company he founded still dances Revelations today.
