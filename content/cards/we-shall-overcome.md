"We Shall Overcome" is the anthem of the American civil rights movement, and its modern version was born on a picket line in Charleston, South Carolina. On 22 October 1945 more than a thousand workers at the American Tobacco Company's cigar factory walked out, demanding higher pay. Black and white strikers picketed through the winter. One of the Black strikers, Lucille Simmons, took an old hymn and turned "I" into "we": "We will overcome." The song came to close each day of picketing, and the strikers added verses such as "We will win our rights".

The strike ended in April 1946. Two of its members, Anna Lee Bonneau and Evelyn Risher, then took the song to the Highlander Folk School in Monteagle, Tennessee, and taught it to Zilphia Horton. She passed it to the folk singer Pete Seeger, and by 1952 New York singers had recorded it as "We Shall Overcome". In April 1960 Guy Carawan of Highlander led the students founding SNCC in Raleigh in singing it, and the movement made it its own. On 15 March 1965 President Lyndon Johnson, asking Congress for a voting rights law, said: "And we shall overcome." The words a tobacco worker had changed on a picket line reached the floor of Congress.
