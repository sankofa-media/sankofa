In September 1829 David Walker, a free Black clothes dealer in Boston, published a pamphlet urging the enslaved in the American South to resist. Born in Wilmington, North Carolina, to a free mother, he kept a clothing business on Brattle Street and was an agent for [[freedoms-journal|Freedom's Journal]], the country's first Black newspaper. His Appeal ... to the Coloured Citizens of the World ran to three editions by June 1830, and he asked sailors heading south to carry copies. Where copies surfaced in Southern ports, authorities seized them, and Georgia, North Carolina and other states passed laws against antislavery writing and against teaching Black people to read. According to Henry Highland Garnet, Georgians put a price on Walker's head; the sums reported differ. Walker died in Boston in 1830. Rumour said he was killed; official records gave consumption.
