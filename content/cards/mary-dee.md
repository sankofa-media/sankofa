WHOD was a 250-watt station that went on the air in 1948 in Homestead, a town just outside Pittsburgh. It was built to serve the region's blue-collar immigrants with ethnic and foreign-language programmes.

Among the voices it launched was Mary Dee Dudley, a disc jockey whom Pittsburgh remembers as the first Black woman to hold that job in the United States. Alongside her the station's Porky Chedwick played records by Black artists, and in the late 1940s the little Homestead station became the place where Pittsburgh heard Black music and Black news on the air.

In 1956 WHOD gave up its programmes for immigrants and became WAMO, the station that went on to launch further careers in Black radio in Pittsburgh.

A station built for immigrant listeners thus became the cradle of Black radio in western Pennsylvania, and a Black woman with her own show on it in 1948 showed that the microphone did not have to belong to one kind of voice.
