Soul Train was an American television dance show that ran from 1970 to 2006 and put Black music, dancers and style on screens across the country. Its creator, Don Cornelius, had sold tyres and insurance and worked as a Chicago police officer before he turned to broadcasting: he was an announcer and news reporter at the radio station WVON, then a news and sports reporter at the UHF station WCIU. He paid for the pilot with his own money.

On 17 August 1970 Soul Train went on the air on WCIU as a local show. George Johnson, head of Johnson Products, a Black-owned maker of hair products, backed it, and from 2 October 1971 it was syndicated to stations nationwide and made in Los Angeles. Its signature became the Soul Train Line: two rows of dancers with an aisle between them, down which pairs strutted their best moves. Cornelius closed every show wishing viewers love, peace and soul.

He stepped down as host in 1993 but went on producing until new episodes ended in 2006, which made Soul Train one of the longest-running syndicated programmes in American television. Cornelius took his own life in 2012. Week after week, his show had let young Black Americans dance, dress and wear their hair their own way in front of the country.
