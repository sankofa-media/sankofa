In 2009 the French-Senegalese filmmaker Mati Diop made a short documentary in Dakar, Atlantiques, about young men setting out in a small fishing boat for Spain in search of work. Ten years later the same subject became her first feature, and this time she turned to the women who stay behind.

On the Dakar shore a futuristic tower is going up. Seventeen-year-old Ada loves Souleiman, one of its builders, but she has been promised to a rich man. The workers have not been paid for weeks, and one night Souleiman and his comrades set off by boat for Spain. Then a fire ruins Ada's wedding, a strange fever spreads, and possessed women with milk-white eyes come to the developer to demand the wages owed to the men who left. Shot in Wolof, it is a ghost story, a love story and a story about migration at once.

In May 2019 Diop became the first Black woman director in the Cannes competition, and the film won the Grand Prix. Her uncle, Djibril Diop Mambéty, had filmed young Dakar lovers dreaming of France in [[touki-bouki|Touki Bouki]] in 1973, and in 2013 she paid tribute to it in A Thousand Suns. Two generations of one family had now filmed the urge to leave.
