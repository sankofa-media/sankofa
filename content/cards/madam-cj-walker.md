Around 1900 scalp disease, poor diet and poor hygiene left many Black American women with brittle hair and hair loss. Sarah Breedlove was born on 23 December 1867 near Delta, Louisiana, on the cotton plantation where her parents had been enslaved. Orphaned at seven and married at fourteen, she was widowed in 1887 with a small daughter, Lelia, and moved to St Louis, where her brothers were barbers and she worked as a laundress.

Losing her own hair, she began selling hair preparations. In 1905 she moved to Denver; there she married Charles J. Walker, renamed herself Madam C. J. Walker and sold her own Wonderful Hair Grower. In 1910 she set up a laboratory and beauty school in Indianapolis. Her products were sold by thousands of Black women agents across the country.

She gave money to Black institutions and to the NAACP and spoke out against lynching. She died on 25 May 1919 at Villa Lewaro, her mansion at Irvington-on-Hudson. Her daughter, A'Lelia Walker, went on to become a central figure of the Harlem Renaissance.
