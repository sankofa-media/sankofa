Losing Ground is a 1982 feature film by Kathleen Collins, an African American writer and filmmaker and one of the first Black women to direct a narrative feature. It is the story of a marriage. Sara Rogers, played by Seret Scott, is a philosophy professor researching the philosophical and religious meaning of ecstatic experience. Her husband Victor, a painter played by Bill Gunn, has just sold a painting to a museum and rents a house in the country for the summer. Their idyll grows complicated as Sara tries not only to study ecstasy but to find it for herself, and the marriage begins to unravel.

In 1982 the film played at festivals but no one picked it up for release in cinemas. Collins struggled to find money and distribution for her work, and she died in 1988, at 46; most of what she made went unseen for years.

Her daughter, Nina Collins, raised money to restore Losing Ground. The restoration was completed in 2015 and Milestone Films released the film, and in 2020 the Library of Congress added it to the National Film Registry. A film made in the early 1980s by and about a Black woman intellectual had at last reached its audience.
