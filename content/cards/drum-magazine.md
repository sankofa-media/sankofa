Drum was a magazine for Black South Africans that took off in Johannesburg in 1951, in the early years of apartheid. Its new owner, Jim Bailey, and his editor, Anthony Sampson, filled it with [[jazz|jazz]], sport and city life, and gave photography pride of place in a country that had no picture magazine of its own. Sophiatown, a mixed neighbourhood on the edge of the city, supplied many of its stories, until the government cleared it for a whites-only suburb.

Its most celebrated reporter was Henry Nxumalo, known to readers as "Mr Drum". To expose the potato farms of Bethal, where labourers were beaten and kept in miserable conditions, he went there disguised as a worker, and the photographer Jürgen Schadeberg's pictures backed his story; the 1952 issue sold out. Another time Nxumalo got himself arrested so that he could describe prison conditions from the inside.

On New Year's Eve 1957, by most accounts, Nxumalo was stabbed to death while investigating an abortion racket.

Undercover investigative reporting of this kind had not existed in South Africa before Drum, and Nxumalo is remembered as its pioneer. The title survived him: it is still published, now by the media group Media24.
