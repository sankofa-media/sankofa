The Nazi government meant the Berlin Olympics of 1936 to show off its idea of "Aryan" supremacy. The American team included 18 African Americans. One of them, the sprinter and long jumper Jesse Owens, won four gold medals: the 100 metres, the 200 metres, the long jump and the 4×100 metres relay.

A story soon spread that Hitler, enraged, had refused to shake the Black champion's hand. Hitler did not shake it, but Owens later said the two of them had exchanged congratulatory waves. What hurt him came from home. President Franklin D. Roosevelt never invited him to the White House and never publicly acknowledged his victories. It was the president, not Hitler, Owens said, who had snubbed him: "The president didn't even send me a telegram."

Back home, Olympic gold did not turn into work, and Owens struggled for money.

The legend puts the insult in Berlin; the athlete himself placed it in Washington.
