The Merikins are a community in the south of Trinidad descended from African Americans who won their freedom in the War of 1812, fought between Britain and the United States. When the British fleet came into Chesapeake Bay, enslaved people from Maryland and Virginia, and later from Georgia, escaped to its ships. Many of the men enlisted in the Corps of Colonial Marines, six companies that fought against their former masters.

When the war ended, they were garrisoned in Bermuda, and Britain wanted to move these seasoned men into the West India Regiments of the regular army. The marines refused. Again and again they held the government to the bargain they had struck: settlement as free people in a British colony.

In 1815 and 1816 more than seven hundred men, women and children were landed in Trinidad, where most people of African descent would stay enslaved until 1838. They were given forest land around the old mission of Savanna Grande, now Princes Town, each company in its own village. The area is still known as the Company Villages.

People called them Merikins, a Creole way of saying "American". Two centuries on, their descendants still bear the name, a memory of men who bargained with an empire and won.
