The Dogon of Mali live along the Bandiagara escarpment, a towering cliff with villages set into its rock face. Every sixty years they hold the Sigui, a cycle of ceremonies of death and renewal; the last began in 1967.

From 1931 the French anthropologist Marcel Griaule worked among the Dogon, later with Germaine Dieterlen. In 1950 the two reported that Dogon tradition named a companion of Sirius, po tolo, a star invisible to the naked eye, and credited it with enormous mass. In 1976 the writer Robert Temple went further: in The Sirius Mystery he argued that amphibious beings from the Sirius system had taught the Dogon.

In 1991 the anthropologist Walter van Beek published a restudy. The Dogon he worked with knew Sirius as a star, the brightest in the sky, but no one outside Griaule's circle of informants had heard of po tolo, and no one, even inside it, had heard that Sirius was double. Stars, he found, mattered little in Dogon ritual. He suggested that Griaule, in long sessions with one main informant, had read his own knowledge of Sirius into the answers.

Meanwhile the Dogon's own great ceremony keeps its count: sixty years after 1967, the Sigui is due again around 2027.
