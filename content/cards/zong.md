In late 1781 the Liverpool-owned slave ship Zong, carrying about twice as many captives as it was built for, overshot Jamaica. Its master, Luke Collingwood, a surgeon by training, and the crew threw more than 130 Africans into the sea. The crew later claimed the drinking water was running out, yet some of the killings came after rain had replenished it. The owners claimed the dead on insurance as lost cargo.

In 1783 a jury found for the owners. [[equiano|Olaudah Equiano]] carried the news to [[granville-sharp|Granville Sharp]], who demanded a murder trial and never got one. In May Lord Mansfield's court granted the insurers a new trial; there is no evidence it was ever held.

Outrage over the killings fed the abolition society founded in 1787, and abolitionists went on to make the slave ship itself an image in the [[brookes|Brookes]] print. In 2008 M. NourbeSe Philip built a poem, Zong!, only from the words of the case report, Gregson v. Gilbert.
