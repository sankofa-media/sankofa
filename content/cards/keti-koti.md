Keti Koti, "the chains are broken", is 1 July, the day on which Suriname and the Netherlands mark the end of slavery in the Dutch colonies of the Atlantic. On 1 July 1863 the Netherlands abolished slavery in Suriname and on its Caribbean islands, one of the last colonial powers to do so.

Freedom came with conditions. The debate in parliament turned less on human rights than on money, and the state paid the slaveholders 300 guilders for every enslaved person in Suriname; the compensation went to the owners. In Suriname, though not on the islands, the people freed from the plantations had to keep working there for another ten years, for wages but under state supervision. That obligation ended only in 1873, which is why 1873 is often called the real end of Dutch slavery.

The Dutch role in slavery was long downplayed and barely taught in schools. Since 2002 the national commemoration has been held in Amsterdam's Oosterpark, at a bronze monument by the Surinamese artist Erwin de Vries. On 1 July 2023, 150 years after 1873, King Willem-Alexander stood at that monument, apologised for the Dutch role in slavery and asked forgiveness.
