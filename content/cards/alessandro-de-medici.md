In the early sixteenth century Florence still called itself a republic, though the Medici banking family had long ruled it from behind the scenes and avoided princely titles. Alessandro de' Medici was born around 1511, the illegitimate son of a servant in the Medici household, usually identified as Simonetta da Collevecchio. Officially his father was Lorenzo de' Medici, Duke of Urbino; many believed it was Giulio de' Medici, the future Pope Clement VII.

People talked about his mother. Some contemporaries called her a slave, and Alessandro was nicknamed "il Moro", the Moor. What that meant is still debated: "Moor" could describe a North African, an Arab or simply a dark-skinned man, and some historians see her as a peasant woman from Lazio rather than an African.

A bargain between Pope Clement and the Emperor Charles V made Alessandro the first hereditary Duke of Florence in the early 1530s, ending the pretence of a republic. On 6 January 1537 he was murdered by his cousin Lorenzino. His small daughter Giulia was taken in by Maria Salviati, mother of the future Grand Duke Cosimo I, and Pontormo painted the two of them together.

His portraits and the rumours about him show a Renaissance court where a ruler's possible African ancestry was whispered about, painted and argued over, as it still is today.
