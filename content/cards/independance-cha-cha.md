"Indépendance Cha Cha" is a song of 1960 in the style of Congolese rumba, the dance music of Léopoldville that had grown out of Cuban records. Its band, African Jazz, was led by the singer Joseph Kabasele, known as Le Grand Kallé, the biggest name in Congolese music. That year the Belgian Congo was negotiating its freedom.

In January 1960 Congolese politicians gathered in Brussels for the Round Table talks with Belgium. Homesick for their own music, the delegates had Kabasele's band brought over to entertain them; two musicians from the rival OK Jazz, among them the singer Vicky Longomba, joined the trip. Playing for the delegates at the Hotel Plaza, where they stayed, the band produced a new song, written on the spot and recorded in Brussels. Its lyrics hail independence and name the politicians themselves, rivals such as Kasa-Vubu and Lumumba side by side.

Congo became independent on 30 June 1960, and the song travelled further still. As one African country after another became independent that year and after, "Indépendance Cha Cha" became a hit across the continent. In Congo it is still heard as the sound of independence itself, closer to people's hearts than any official anthem.
