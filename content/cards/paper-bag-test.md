The paper bag test is a story about colorism, the preference for lighter skin, told in Black communities in the United States: hold a brown paper bag to someone's arm, and if their skin is darker, they are not let into the club, the fraternity or the sorority.

The scholar Audrey Elisa Kerr set out to ask Black Washington whether the test was real or an urban myth. Her book The Paper Bag Principle, built largely on oral history, records the rumours and legends themselves rather than settling them. Whatever became of the bag, the preference it names can be measured.

In North Carolina, a 2011 Villanova study of more than 12,000 imprisoned Black women found that those perceived as light-skinned got sentences about 12% shorter and served about 11% less time, even after accounting for the kind of crime and their criminal records.
