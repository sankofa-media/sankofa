Sranan Tongo is the common language of Suriname, on the north-eastern coast of South America. Almost everyone in the country speaks it, as a mother tongue or a second language, and it is also heard among the large Surinamese community in the Netherlands. It is an English-based creole made by enslaved Africans, and Dutch, the official language, has left its own mark on it.

It began on the plantations and in the town of Paramaribo in the late 17th century. English colonists settled Suriname in 1651, and the enslaved Africans brought to their plantations built a new language out of the colonists' English and their own speech. In 1667 the Dutch seized the colony, and most of the English planters soon left. Their language did not leave with them. The Dutch kept the local English-based vernacular, rather than their own, as the language for talking with the enslaved.

Sranan went on to give birth to others. People who escaped the plantations carried early Sranan into the rainforest, where it grew into new maroon languages such as [[saramaccan|Saramaccan]]. On the coast it stayed the shared speech of a country of many peoples, and though it long ranked below Dutch, it has become a sign of being Surinamese.
