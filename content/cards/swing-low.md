"Swing Low, Sweet Chariot" is a [[spirituals|spiritual]], one of the sacred songs of enslaved African Americans. The account most often cited takes it to Indian Territory, today Oklahoma, in the mid-nineteenth century. Wallace Willis and his wife Minerva were enslaved by Britt Willis, a Choctaw, who hired them out to Spencer Academy, a school for Choctaw boys. In the evenings the couple sang their own songs to the pupils.

The school's superintendent, Alexander Reid, did not forget them. In 1871 the Jubilee Singers of Fisk University in Nashville set out on a tour to raise money for their school. When Reid heard them at a concert in New Jersey, he wrote down the Willis songs and passed them on to the singers. The Jubilee Singers made "Swing Low" famous.

More than a century later it became the song of English rugby. On 19 March 1988, as the story goes, the Twickenham crowd sang it when the winger Chris Oti scored a hat-trick against Ireland. In June 2020, as Black Lives Matter protests spread, the Rugby Football Union began a review of the song, saying that many fans had no idea of its origins.
