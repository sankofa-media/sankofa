Around 1760 the Swedish court acquired an unusual new member. A boy named Couschi, born around 1747, probably on the Caribbean island of Saint Croix, was brought to Stockholm and given to Queen Lovisa Ulrika.

The queen tried an experiment: she let the child grow up in freedom, without the usual schooling and discipline. Later he was given religious instruction, and in December 1768 he was baptised in the palace chapel with the royal family standing as godparents. From them he took the names Adolf Ludvig Gustav Fredrik Albert, and he was known as Badin.

He served the queen until her death in 1782 and then Princess Sofia Albertina. He danced and acted on stage, joined the Freemasons and the order Par Bricole, married twice and collected a large private library. One of his notebooks is kept at Uppsala University Library.

Badin died in Stockholm in March 1822, a free man. He had arrived as a gift; he left behind his own books, his own orders and a notebook in his own hand.
