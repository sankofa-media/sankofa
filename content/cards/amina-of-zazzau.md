Zazzau, today Zaria in northern Nigeria, was one of the city-states of Hausaland. Its most famous ruler is Amina, a queen who by most accounts lived in the 16th century.

According to tradition she reigned for 34 years and spent them at war. The chronicles credit her with conquests far to the south and say that she brought the neighbouring Hausa states of Kano and Katsina under her rule. She is also credited with surrounding the towns she conquered with walls, some of which survive. By the usual account she died on campaign, at a place called Atagara.

Almost all of this was written down long after her lifetime. The scholar-ruler Muhammad Bello wrote about her in the 19th century, and chronicles and oral traditions supply the rest. Historians disagree about her dates, and a few have argued that she never existed. None of this has dimmed her: Hausa praise remembers her as a woman as capable as a man, and she is still remembered, above all, as the warrior queen.
