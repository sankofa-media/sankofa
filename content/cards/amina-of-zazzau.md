Zazzau, today Zaria in northern Nigeria, was one of the city-states of Hausaland. Its most famous ruler is Amina, a queen who by most accounts lived in the 16th century.

According to tradition she reigned for 34 years and spent them at war. The Kano Chronicle says that she conquered all the towns as far as Nupe and Kwararafa, far to the south, that every town paid her tribute, and that the ruler of Nupe sent her eunuchs and kola nuts. She is also credited with surrounding the towns she conquered with walls, some of which survive. By the usual account she died on campaign, at a place called Atagara.

Almost all of this was written down long after her lifetime. The Kano Chronicle mentions her, scholars of the 19th-century Sokoto Caliphate such as Sultan Muhammad Bello wrote of her, and oral traditions supply the rest. Historians disagree about her dates, and a few have argued that she never existed. None of this has dimmed her: Hausa praise remembers her as a woman as capable as a man, and she is still remembered, above all, as the warrior queen.
