In the late 15th century the Songhai empire, centred on Gao on the middle Niger, was the great power of the western Sahel. Its great conqueror, Sonni Ali, died in 1492. In April 1493 Muhammad Ture defeated Ali's son and heir, Sonni Baru, took the throne and adopted the title askia; it became the name of his dynasty.

He made Islam the religion of the state. In 1496 he set off on pilgrimage to Mecca and came home with the title of caliph. He then rebuilt the empire as a state: provinces under governors, a professional army, a fleet of war canoes on the river. His campaigns reached west towards the Atlantic and east into the Hausa states.

His sons ended his reign. In 1528, old and nearly blind, he was deposed and his eldest son took the throne as Askia Musa; later the old man was exiled to an island in the Niger. In 1537 another son, Ismail, came to power and brought him back to Gao, where he died in 1538.

He was buried in Gao beneath the earthen pyramid he had built, which still stands. His reign is told in two chronicles of the Songhai world, the Tarikh al-Sudan and the Tarikh al-Fattash.
