The Migration Series is a set of sixty small tempera paintings by Jacob Lawrence, made in Harlem in 1940–41. It tells the story of the Great Migration, the movement of Black Southerners to the cities of the North that began during the First World War. The first caption reads: "During the World War there was a great migration north by southern Negroes."

Lawrence was born in Atlantic City, New Jersey, in 1917 and came of age in Harlem, where he studied under Charles Alston at the Harlem Art Workshop and came to know artists such as [[the-harp-savage|Augusta Savage]]. A grant from the Julius Rosenwald Fund let him take on the subject. He wrote a caption for each panel and conceived the sixty as one unit, a single story to be read in order.

He was 23. In late 1941 the series was shown at the Downtown Gallery in New York. Then the set was divided: in 1942 the Museum of Modern Art bought the thirty even-numbered panels, and the Phillips Collection in Washington bought the odd-numbered ones. The halves have since been reunited for exhibitions, in Washington in 2008 and at MoMA in 2015.

In flat, bright colour, panel by panel, the journeys of ordinary families became one of the great narrative works of American painting.
