Aksum, in the highlands of northern Ethiopia, was the capital of a great trading empire in the first centuries AD, trading through the Red Sea port of Adulis. The graves of its rulers and nobles were marked by huge carved monoliths. Of the three colossal stelae in the royal cemetery, meant to stand in a line, only one was still upright in modern times. The one now called the Aksum Obelisk, about 24 metres tall and more than 150 tonnes, had fallen long before and lay on the ground.

In 1937, during the Italian occupation, Mussolini's forces took it to Rome as a war trophy and set it up in front of the building now used by the UN Food and Agriculture Organization. After the war Ethiopia asked for it back; in the peace treaty of 1947 Italy undertook to return what it had looted, yet for decades the obelisk stayed where it was. In May 2002 lightning struck its top.

In 2005 the obelisk, taken apart into three sections, was flown back to Aksum. In 2008, in a project run by UNESCO with Italian money and Ethiopian partners, it was raised again on its original site. For Ethiopians it was a symbol of their history, and its return closed a dispute that had lasted since the war.
