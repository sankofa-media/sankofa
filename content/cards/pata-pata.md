"Pata Pata" is a song Miriam Makeba brought from Johannesburg, and in 1967 it became an American hit. Makeba, born in Johannesburg in 1932, sang in the 1950s with an all-woman group, the Skylarks, and with them she first recorded the song at home, in a country governed by apartheid.

In 1959 a part in Come Back, Africa, an American anti-apartheid documentary, brought her to notice abroad, and with Harry Belafonte's help she settled in the United States. In 1960 South Africa refused to let her back in, and in 1963 it banned her records.

In 1967 she recorded "Pata Pata" again for the Reprise label with the producer Jerry Ragovoy, brighter and faster than before. The single climbed into the Billboard top 20 and carried her voice far beyond the audiences that already knew her.

Makeba stayed in exile for some thirty years. In 1990 the newly freed Nelson Mandela urged her to come home, and she did. She died in 2008 in Italy, shortly after a concert. The song she had first sung as a young woman in Johannesburg had kept her voice in the world's ear through all the years she could not go home.
