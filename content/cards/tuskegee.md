From 1932 to 1972 a US Public Health Service study in Macon County, Alabama, withheld effective treatment from Black men with syphilis. It enrolled 600 men: 399 with syphilis, 201 without. They were told they were being treated for "bad blood", lured with free meals and burial insurance, and never asked for consent. When penicillin became the standard cure, they were not offered it. In 1966 Peter Buxtun, a Service employee in San Francisco, wrote to the CDC objecting, and was rebuffed for years. In 1972 he gave documents to the Associated Press; Jean Heller's story ran on 25 July, and the study ended that year. A lawsuit won survivors and heirs $10 million. The 1974 National Research Act made review boards vet federally funded research on people. In 1997 President Clinton apologised; the last participant died in 2004.
