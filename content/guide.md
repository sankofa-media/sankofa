---
title: How to read this museum
---

Sankofa is a museum you can enter anywhere. There is a story to read from top to bottom, but every page also opens onto other pages, and no two readers leave by the same door. This is how it is put together.

## Chapters and cards

The story runs through eight chapters, from Africa before the slave ships to the starships of Afrofuturism. Each chapter is told in a few steps over a map and a picture, and each step leans on cards. A card is the museum's unit: a few lines you can take in at a glance, one thing that should surprise you, the sources at the foot, and the roads that lead further. The museum now holds {{cards}} cards. Open one and it slides in beside the story; its links open the next.

## Three books

The cards come from three books, as in Milorad Pavić's *Dictionary of the Khazars*, where three books tell one story and do not agree. The Book of Voices ❝ is history told by the people who lived it: narratives, testimony, songs, interviews. The Book of Archives ▤ is the same world in the ledgers of those who traded and ruled it: ship logs, insurance claims, laws, bills of sale. The Book of Heirs ✶ is the diaspora's art, memory and argument. Every card carries the mark of its book, and every link carries the mark of the book it leads to, so you can follow one book through the whole museum, or deliberately cross from one to another and watch the accounts disagree.

## Two copies

Pavić's dictionary exists in a male and a female copy that differ in a single passage. This museum has two copies too: when you first arrive you are dealt one, at random, and your browser keeps it. Both hold the whole dictionary. Every card is in each copy, on the atlas, among the countries, in the index and in the search, and a link always opens the very card it names. What differs is the telling. The two copies tell the same themes, one with a man's view and one with a woman's: some cards read differently in each, and some chapter steps tell their theme through another life. In one copy the chapter has [[douglass-covey|Frederick Douglass]] fight back against the man hired to break him, in the other [[jacobs-garret|Harriet Jacobs]] hides for nearly seven years under a roof. There are now {{pairs}} such pairs, and each card of a pair names the card that tells its theme in the other copy. The note on the copies at the start of the story says which copy you hold and lists the pairs; it also has a button that opens the other copy.

## Your book

Every card you open is written into your own book, in your browser and nowhere else. The book in the top bar shows the path you walked; share it, and someone else can walk the same road or replay it on the atlas.

## The atlas and the descent

Every card has a place and a time. The atlas shows them on a map with a timeline below it, and the same point can be opened in the terminal: a card's "Open in terminal" gives the exact command. The descent goes the other way, down through the centuries from 1300 to today, while the sky changes colour with the years. Both share five levels of detail. At the first you see centuries and the great movements; at the last, a single document. Choose the level at the top.

## Roots and the music map

Roots answers a simple question: where does your music come from? Type a band or an artist and a chain runs back from them, link by link, to Black traditions, ending at a card of the museum. Every link is documented by two sources. The music map draws all the chains at once, with later artists higher up; touch one and its way down to the roots lights up.

## The listening room

The museum runs on a real Kubernetes cluster, and the listening room lets you hear it. A bell keeps time for the machine that runs the cluster; every other machine plays a drum as long as its heartbeat arrives on time; the cluster calls its programs and they answer. When a machine falls silent you hear the hole it leaves. If the live stream cannot be reached, a recorded performance plays instead, and the room says so.

## The engine room

Every card is also an object in that cluster, and the cluster's own machinery is named after the culture it serves: Griot remembers the life of every object, Kgotla holds a council before anything protected is deleted, Polyrhythm spreads scheduled jobs across free beats. The engine room shows the card you opened last as the cluster stores it, and explains how a visitor can read the museum's cluster with a read-only account.

## Documents, the Workshop and the cabinet

Some cards open onto a primary document lying on a desk, such as a page of [[equiano|Equiano]], of [[douglass-covey|Douglass]], of [[jacobs-garret|Jacobs]], the first issue of *[[freedoms-journal|Freedom's Journal]]*. As you read, the lines in question are outlined on the scan and explained one by one. There are {{documents}} documents so far, each reached from its card. Other cards carry a working object, something to play rather than look at. The Workshop in the top bar gathers them, {{objects}} so far, each one an element that music carries from style to style: the record that lets music travel without people, the rhythm a machine keeps, the break and the sample, the clave, call and response. Each says where and when it was made, by whom, where it went next, and which thread of Roots it follows. Sound starts only when you press something, and only one thing plays at a time.

The museum also has hidden caches. What you find in them goes into your own Cabinet of Curiosities, kept in your browser like your book; nothing you need to read the museum lies only there.

## How we checked

At the foot of every card a panel shows how its facts were checked. The rule is strict: every fact was checked by two independent agents, each against two independent authoritative sources, and a fact stays only if both confirm it. Open a fact to see both verdicts and the sources behind them; the pictures list their licences, copied word for word from their source pages. The page on the method gives the totals, counted afresh every time the museum is built.
